## Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.**

This is an unusually complete plan. Its counts, economy arithmetic and unlock graph agree across documents:

- 3 × (800 + 1,600) = 7,200 credits to fully upgrade a car.
- 800–1,300 credits per standard finish, so 5.5–9 finishes per car, which matches "about eight".
- 3 starters + 9 unlocks = 12 cars.
- Field arithmetic: 1 human + 5 Classic + 2 Roadstormers; 4 humans + 2 + 2.

The architecture choices are sound and the licensing posture is careful. An unfamiliar implementation agent could start M0–M3 today.

However, several **shared contracts** that multiple agents would build against are missing or contradictory:

- the reference driver/pace definition;
- the tactical-intent layer and how difficulty applies to it;
- race end-state rules;
- per-player settings ownership.

The **milestone graph** also lets an open-ended research gate (M4B) block all content work, and trains models before handling is frozen. Some **subjective gates** have no one who can actually close them. **Provenance of LLM-authored code** is weaker than provenance of assets. None of these is a blocker; each needs a document decision before the affected milestone starts.

My confidence is moderate. Learned Roadstormers are a credible but unproven research outcome. The plan gates them properly, but no one can confirm their feasibility until the bridge/training pilot runs (Experiment E2).

## Ranked findings

### F1. "Reference pace" and "reference driver" are never defined, but six systems are calibrated against them

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:**
  - GAME_DESIGN L93–L97: difficulty as "% of validated safe reference pace … relative to a documented reference driver".
  - GAME_DESIGN L79: Arcade Rush allowances of 120/110/102% of "a clean stock starter run".
  - GAME_DESIGN L81: medals "for a stock balanced starter".
  - GAME_DESIGN L63 and PLAN L113: deadline DNFs "against reference player paces".
  - PLAN L76: "a standard assisted driver".
  - OPPONENT_AI L70: learned pace "within approximately 10% of the matched Classic driver".
- **Problem:**
  - No document says who or what produces the reference: an optimizer bot, the Classic AI, or a human.
  - No document gives the car, upgrade and assist settings it uses, or whether "pace %" means a fraction of a speed profile or of lap time. The two differ: 90% speed is roughly 111% lap time.
  - If the reference is the Classic AI, "Expert 100–103%" is circular.
  - If the reference is a physically optimal line, anything above 100% is infeasible by definition ("where physically achievable" only hedges this).
  - The definition must be re-derived whenever handling changes, and no process says so.
- **Failure example:** The M4 agent defines the reference as Classic Expert at 100%. The M7.5 agent computes Arcade Rush timers from a speed-profile optimizer that brakes at the physical limit. Expert Arcade Rush (102% of a superhuman lap) becomes unwinnable for people, while Classic Expert is slower than the "reference" it is supposed to match.
- **Correction:** Add a *Reference Driver* contract to TECHNICAL_DESIGN, with the following parts:
  1. A deterministic offline line-and-speed optimizer. It uses each car's measured grip, brake and acceleration envelope from motor telemetry, stock configuration, default assists and grip-line driving, and computes a per-course, per-car reference speed profile and lap time.
  2. Store these in versioned course data keyed by handling revision. Regenerate and re-check them on every handling revision.
  3. State explicitly that difficulty pace is a *fraction of the reference speed profile*.
  4. Make Expert ≤ 100% of the profile; Expert gains come from later braking points and fewer mistakes within that profile.
  5. Derive Arcade Rush timers, medals and DNF bands from the reference lap scaled by *measured human lap ratios* from the M1/M5 playtests (see F7), not from the bot lap alone.
  6. Define "matched Classic driver" in OPPONENT_AI as same car, same difficulty, same scenario seed.
- **Affected milestones:** M1, M4, M4B, M6, M7.5, M8.1.
- **Verification:** a per-course reference-lap regression test; Classic laps per difficulty must land within their declared bands; a recorded human lap sample per course before Arcade Rush and medal values are finalized.

### F2. M4B gates the whole project with no exit, and trains before handling is frozen

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:**
  - PLAN L117 (M4B "Dependency: M4 and stable handling/content revisions").
  - PLAN L131 (M5 depends on M4B).
  - PLAN L134 (M5.2 finishes the starter cars' handling *after* M4B).
  - PLAN L166, L174, L180 (M7.7 trains; M8.1 then retunes car parity, drift and AI; M8.7 closes).
  - OPPONENT_AI L5 ("continue the training/design milestone rather than relabeling").
  - OPPONENT_AI L51 ("A simulator/handling revision invalidates affected training/evaluation results and requires retraining").
- **Problem:**
  1. M5, M6, M7 and M8 all sit behind M4B, and M4B has no time box, compute ceiling or escalation path. A research stall therefore stops all content and progression work.
  2. Handling legitimately changes in M5.2, as each course lands in M7, and in M8.1. Under OPPONENT_AI L51, each change invalidates the trained checkpoints. The plan has no handling-freeze point and no tolerance for "compatible" small revisions. Training therefore repeats at every tuning pass, the most expensive recurring cost in the plan.
  3. The plan caps only the *pilot* (OPPONENT_AI L45). The total M4B + M7.7 + M8 training budget is unbounded.
- **Failure example:** After three pilot iterations, Surge still differs from Classic in under 25% of windows. M5 cannot start, so no art, audio or progression work proceeds. Alternatively, M8.1 shortens Prism's drift recovery by 10%, which invalidates the M7.7 checkpoints, forces a retrain and a fresh M8.7 evaluation, and any further tuning repeats the loop.
- **Correction (milestone and dependency changes; product scope unchanged):**
  - **(a)** Split M4B.
    - **M4B-a:** contract, bridge, throughput and BC/PPO pilot, starting right after M4 and running *in parallel* with M5 content work.
    - **M5** then requires learned inference running in races with the best pilot checkpoint and passing the safety, fairness and completion gates.
    - **The behavioral-distinction and style gates** become a named gate that must pass before M7.7.
  - **(b)** Add an explicit **handling freeze** at the end of M7 car production, before final training. After the freeze, motor, car and surface changes must pass a *compatibility check*: re-run the frozen checkpoint's completion, contact, pace and intervention gates. Retrain only when that check fails.
  - **(c)** Set a total training budget (CPU/GPU-hours and iterations). When it is exhausted without passing, the coordinator writes a decision record for the owner. Any resulting scope change is the owner's decision. Non-scope fallbacks, such as a different learning algorithm (see Optional improvements), come first.
- **Affected milestones:** M4B, M5, M7.7, M8.1, M8.7.
- **Verification:** an updated dependency graph; a compatibility test that runs a frozen checkpoint against a deliberately perturbed handling revision and reports gate deltas.

### F3. The tactical-intent hierarchy is not required when Classic AI is built, and difficulty is undefined for it

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:**
  - PLAN L108 (M4.2 builds Classic AI "through the player command interface").
  - PLAN L119 (M4B.1 *then* freezes the "shared observation/intent contract").
  - PLAN L123 (M4B.5 requires "difficulty settings" for the learned styles).
  - OPPONENT_AI L19–L25 (observation builder → `TacticalIntent` → safety layer → shared path follower; tactical decisions at 5 Hz).
  - OPPONENT_AI L37 (behavioral cloning from Classic demonstrations).
  - GAME_DESIGN L93–L95 (reaction delays of 300/200/120 ms).
  - TECHNICAL_DESIGN L41–L56 (the runtime ownership table has no ObservationBuilder, TacticalIntent, SafetyLayer or PathFollower).
  - OPPONENT_AI never mentions difficulty (no occurrence of the word).
- **Problem:**
  1. M4's text permits a monolithic Classic planner that emits `DriverCommand`s directly. Behavioral cloning needs Classic demonstrations *in the TacticalIntent action space*. If Classic is not built on that contract, M4B must reverse-engineer intents or rewrite Classic, which invalidates M4's tuning and its 100-race evidence.
  2. With 5 Hz tactics, the decision period alone adds 0–200 ms of latency (about 100 ms on average). Expert's "120 ms reaction delay" then behaves like about 220 ms on average, roughly Racer's nominal value. The documents never say which layer the delay applies to: observation, tactical decision or emergency braking.
  3. No document says how Rookie, Racer and Expert apply to a learned policy. If the policy outputs full-pace intents that are then clamped, it will plan passes the clamped pace cannot complete. Safety corrections will rise and may breach the <5% material-correction gate (OPPONENT_AI L74), or behavior will look incoherent.
  4. SB3's PPO does not support Dict action spaces (verified). The intent mixes continuous values (lane offset, pace) with discrete ones (pass/follow, drift preference), so its encoding must be chosen deliberately.
- **Failure example:** M4 ships a well-tuned direct-command Classic AI. M4B.1 discovers there is no intent stream to imitate and spends its first iteration refactoring Classic. That reopens M4's acceptance. Rookie Roadstormers then fail the intervention gate in M5.
- **Correction:**
  - Define `TacticalIntent`, `ObservationBuilder`, `SafetyLayer` and `PathFollower` in TECHNICAL_DESIGN's ownership table, and require M4.1/M4.2 Classic AI to emit intents only through them.
  - Specify the intent encoding: a Box vector, for example lane offset in [−1, 1] of usable width, pace fraction, and commitment and drift logits thresholded deterministically. Alternatively use a MultiDiscrete scheme.
  - Define reaction delay as a 60 Hz ring-buffer latency on *other cars' observed state*, independent of decision rate. State the effective reaction as delay plus decision latency, set the targets on that basis, and state whether per-tick emergency braking has a separate delay.
  - Condition the learned policy on difficulty, either as an input during training or as separately trained checkpoints, and evaluate every M4B gate at all three difficulties.
- **Affected milestones:** M4, M4B, M5.
- **Verification:** an M4 test that every Classic command passes through the follower; a "lead car brakes" scenario measuring command latency per difficulty; M4B gate reports per difficulty.

### F4. The drift reward is probably imperceptible as specified, and the parity targets are too loose

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L29 (5% per benchmark sweeper; exit surge "up to 4% added acceleration for 0.5 seconds"), L31, L130 (stock car pace "within approximately 5%"); PLAN L76.
- **Problem:**
  1. "4% added acceleration" has no unit or base. If it means 4% of current available acceleration, it does almost nothing. *Assumption:* about 3 m/s² available at a 30 m/s exit. Then 4% is 0.12 m/s² for 0.5 s, about 0.06 m/s (0.2 km/h), worth a few milliseconds. The reward meant to make drifting satisfying would effectively not exist.
  2. Parity is tested on one sweeper with a ±5% band in either direction. A 5% per-corner bias compounds to several seconds over a 60–95 s lap. Hairpins, medium corners, esses and gravel are not distinguished, so one technique can dominate a corner class while the single benchmark passes.
  3. The "clean drift" and "effective grip line" have no defined driver (see F1).
  4. Car parity of ±5% means 3–5 s per lap between cars in "one shared competitive class". At Expert, the slowest cars would never be competitive.
- **Failure example:** An agent implements the surge literally. M1 passes on the sweeper, with drift 4% slower and grip 4% faster. Over six courses, players learn that drifting loses time everywhere except hairpins. That contradicts the core promise that drifting is "competitive with grip".
- **Correction:**
  - Specify the surge as an absolute, bounded quantity (a speed gain in m/s delivered over 0.5 s and capped at top speed), with an intended lap-time contribution. Tune it in M1.
  - Define parity *per corner class* for optimized bot drivers of both styles, using the F1 optimizer. Suggested initial targets, to validate in M1: within ±2% per corner class and ±1% per lap.
  - Back the bot result with a human check (F7).
  - Tighten stock car parity to about ±2% on the mixed reference course, with larger course-specific specialization allowed and documented.
- **Affected milestones:** M1, M7.6, M8.1.
- **Verification:** Experiment E1.

### F5. Race end-states, missed gates and grid order are unspecified, and wait-for-humans mode can promote a slow human

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L57–L63 (grid, ranking, deadline, wait-for-humans, spectator view), L45–L47 (resets); TECHNICAL_DESIGN L96.
- **Problem:** An implementer must invent several essential race rules:
  1. **Finished and retired cars.** No rule says whether a car that has finished, or an AI that has retired, keeps driving, becomes a non-colliding ghost, or despawns. The same applies to a finished human's car while their panel switches to the spectator view. The choice affects collisions with slowing cars past the line, performance, and what remaining racers see.
  2. **Wait-for-humans classification.** In this mode, unfinished AI retire 45 s after the first finish and receive DNF. A human who finishes later is ranked above them, because ranking sorts by finished status first. *Example:* in Rookie solo Career, the human is 3 s behind AI #6 and #7 when the deadline expires. Both retire. The human is classified 6th and scores 3 points instead of 1, which can change podium unlocks.
  3. **Missed gates.** A car that passes outside a gate's span (on a wide run-off, for example) or cuts inside the bounds gets no progress, and nothing tells the player. No rule says how far a gate must extend relative to the drivable corridor. A player can complete a "lap" that does not count and silently lose rank.
  4. **Grid order.** It is given only for career rotation. Quick Race and multiplayer exhibition grids are undefined.
  5. **Finish ties.** Ties resolve by participant ID when two cars cross in the same 60 Hz tick, which systematically favors low IDs.
- **Correction:** Add a "race end-state" subsection to GAME_DESIGN:
  - Finished cars hand over to an AI cool-down drive and stop colliding with racing cars once they leave the finish zone, or are removed after a set distance. Retired AI are removed.
  - In wait-for-humans mode, unfinished AI keep racing until the last human finishes or the five-minute cap expires; DNF is applied only then.
  - Gates span the full legal corridor, run-off included, out to the recovery boundary. If a car advances more than a set distance past a missed required gate, show "Missed checkpoint" and auto-reset it to the anchor before that gate, counting this as a reset.
  - Quick Race and exhibition grid order is seeded, with human positions selectable or defaulting to rotating mid-rear slots.
  - Finish times are interpolated within the tick from the swept gate crossing; participant ID is used only for an exact interpolated tie.
- **Affected milestones:** M4.1, M4.4, M4.5, M6.1.
- **Verification:** constructed T05 scenarios for each rule, including the wait-for-humans classification example above.

### F6. LLM provider terms and dedication authority are not covered for code and recipes, which are most of the project

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:**
  - ASSET_PRODUCTION L28 (provider and model recorded "when available").
  - ASSET_PRODUCTION L30 (a per-contribution dedication statement; an agent must not sign as a person).
  - ASSET_PRODUCTION L103 (provider terms reviewed only for "remote image/audio/model generation").
  - ASSET_PRODUCTION L112 (the origin enum distinguishes `llm_script_generated` from `service_generated`).
  - ASSET_PRODUCTION L119 ("unknown legal origin blocks shipping", which the manifest applies to *assets*).
  - PLAN L212 (task records note provider/model "when available").
- **Problem:**
  1. Every script, recipe, score and test is output from remote LLM services, and each provider's terms govern ownership and use of that output. The plan checks provider terms only for directly service-generated media. The authoring LLMs behind `llm_script_generated` assets and all code are never checked.
  2. Projects like this often rotate among many providers and model routes, including promotional or unannounced models whose terms may be unpublished. Provenance is allowed to say "unknown model", and for code no rule blocks unknown legal origin.
  3. The per-contribution dedication statement has no valid signer. Agents may not sign, and the plan does not say the owner gives a standing dedication. In practice the statements will either be missing or written by agents anyway.
- **Failure example:** The M8.6 audit finds hundreds of commits with no provider/model record and no evidence of which output terms applied. The release statement (ASSET_PRODUCTION L20) nonetheless asserts Unlicense/CC0 for all of that code.
- **Correction:**
  - **(1)** In M0.3, the owner personally approves a standing dedication covering all contributions produced under the owner's direction by registered tools, to the extent the owner holds rights.
  - **(2)** Keep a dated **provider-terms register** covering every LLM provider, model or route used for *any* authoring, code included. Review an entry before the provider's first use. A provider with unknown or incompatible output terms may not author shipped material.
  - **(3)** Require a per-commit provenance trailer (provider, model if known, date, task ID) and enforce it in CI.
  - **(4)** Extend "unknown legal origin blocks shipping" to code, data and model artifacts.
  - The plan's existing caution against promising originality or copyrightability should stay.
- **Affected milestones:** M0.3, M0.5, M0.7, M8.6, M9.4.
- **Verification:** a CI trailer lint; a register review at each milestone close; an M8.6 sampled audit that traces commits to register entries.

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:**

  - PLAN L141 (M5 subjective criteria).
  - PLAN L182 (a release can go ahead with "no independent human playtest" reported as a limitation).

### F8. Per-player settings ownership in local multiplayer, and AI assist levels, are undefined

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L11–L13 (assists), L159 (the profile owns settings; devices are separate from profiles), L182 (per-player camera); PLAN L95 (persistent preferred controls), L149 (M6.3 "career rewards scoped to the selected host profile"); TECHNICAL_DESIGN L108, L116.

### F9. The 3D physics backend is not pinned, and the method for detecting the road surface is unspecified

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** TECHNICAL_DESIGN L3, L62–L70 (custom motor on `CharacterBody3D` with sweeps and probes), L50/L64 ("surface response"); PLAN L55.
- **Problem:**
  - Since Godot 4.6, Jolt has been the default 3D physics engine for new projects (verified). Jolt's documentation notes three relevant behaviors:
    - its collision margins "can sometimes result in odd collision normals when performing shape queries";
    - internal-edge ("ghost collision") handling is configurable;
    - ray-cast `face_index` is −1 by default unless a project setting enables it, which costs about 25% more memory for concave shapes.
  - The plan never names the backend, yet sweeps, chunk-seam behavior and per-triangle surface lookup depend on it.
  - Godot 4.8 is at beta5. A stable 4.8 release may arrive during M0–M2, which makes the "update the pinned version through a recorded decision" clause (PLAN L55) relevant early.
- **Correction:**
  - Record the physics backend as an M0.1/M0.6 decision and treat any later switch as a handling revision.
  - Detect the surface from the track compiler's route-local surface map (arc length × lateral offset) or from per-surface collision bodies, not from triangle indices.
  - Run the M1/M2 seam, oblique-impact and tunneling tests under the chosen backend.
  - Allow engine upgrades only at milestone boundaries, with a full regression run.
- **Affected milestones:** M0, M1, M2.
- **Verification:** an M2 seam test across chunk boundaries at maximum speed; a surface-transition test.

### F10. In multiplayer, it is unclear whose progress triggers a scenic spectacle

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L122 ("Trigger a spectacle from valid route progress with a cooldown … does not … restart per camera"), L107–L112 ("aircraft crossing", "Helicopter tracking a broad bend").
- **Problem:** Up to four humans can be spread around one shared world. No rule says whose progress triggers a spectacle. With a cooldown, trailing players may never see a one-shot event such as the plane crossing. The helicopter's tracking target is also undefined. Memorable scenery and four-player play are both core goals, and this is where they interact.
- **Correction:**
  - Classify spectacles in the track schema.
    - **Ambient loops:** turbines, waterfalls, ships, cranes and cable cars run continuously, phased from the race clock.
    - **Event spectacles:** triggered by each human's first entry into a zone, with a visibility window long enough (for example 6–10 s) for nearby players to share the event, and a cooldown shorter than typical gaps between humans.
  - Tracking spectacles follow the leading human in their zone.
- **Affected milestones:** M5.1, M7.4.
- **Verification:** an M5 four-player capture checklist: each human sees each major spectacle on lap 1 in a stated share of seeded races.

### F11. Bit-identical regeneration hashes across machines are likely to fail as specified (hypothesis)

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** PLAN L89 ("matching normalized outputs and hashes under the pinned toolchain"); TECHNICAL_DESIGN L102.
- **Problem (hypothesis):**
  - Python/numpy synthesis and geometry code can differ in the last floating-point bits between arm64 macOS and x86-64 Linux, because of different libm and SIMD code paths.
  - Godot resources saved by scripts may receive non-deterministic resource UIDs unless the UIDs are assigned explicitly.
  - Pinning versions does not pin CPU architecture.
- **Failure example:** CI flags every audio and mesh asset as changed when a developer regenerates on a Mac, so the M2 acceptance flaps.
- **Correction:**
  - Name one canonical regeneration platform (for example, the CI Linux image) for exact hash equality.
  - On other hosts, compare within declared tolerances: quantized PCM within ±1 LSB, vertices within a stated epsilon.
  - Derive resource UIDs deterministically from stable asset IDs.
- **Affected milestones:** M0.4/M0.5, M2.6.
- **Verification:** in M0, regenerate one mesh and one sound on both architectures and compare.

### F12. Drafting is effectively inert as specified

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L51; drafting is also a Classic personality axis (L97) and part of M4.2.
- **Problem:** Drafting gives a 2% drag reduction that "cannot … exceed the normal maximum-speed allowance". If top speed is a hard cap, drafting provides no closing speed on long straights, which is where it matters. Even uncapped, 2% less drag raises terminal speed by only about 0.7–1%, roughly 1.5–2 km/h at 220 km/h. AI personalities and Roadstormer features would then be built around a mechanic with no observable effect.
- **Correction:** Decide whether drafting is a passing tool. If it is, allow a bounded terminal-speed gain inside the wake and test that it enables but does not guarantee passes. If it is not, drop it from personality and training features.
- **Affected milestones:** M1, M4.2.
- **Verification:** a two-car straight-line scenario that measures closing distance.

### F13. Simulation and inference budgets lack units and a scheduling rule

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** TECHNICAL_DESIGN L132 (a 4 ms simulation budget; "0.5 ms total per decision update" for inference); OPPONENT_AI L27 (fewer than 10,000 parameters), L39 (12-tick decision steps), L73.
- **Problem:**
  - The 4 ms budget does not say whether it applies per physics tick or per rendered frame, or whether it is a mean or a p99.
  - If all eight AI make their 5 Hz decisions on the same tick, that tick spikes. The documents specify no phase-staggering scheme, and training must reproduce whichever scheme runtime uses.
  - *Hypothesis:* a 10k-parameter MLP needs about 10k multiply-adds per inference. In typed GDScript loops that may cost a few tenths of a millisecond per rival, near the 0.5 ms shared by both rivals before observation assembly.
  - The two 32-unit hidden layers the document suggests imply only about 3k parameters unless history inputs are flattened into hundreds of features.
- **Correction:**
  - State budgets per 60 Hz tick as mean and p99.
  - Stagger AI decision phases deterministically by participant index, and mirror that scheme in the training bridge.
  - Set the parameter cap from measured cost (Experiment E5).
- **Affected milestones:** M3.5, M4, M4B.4.
- **Verification:** E5, plus the M3/M8 profiling captures.

### F14. M3 combines the rendering gate with physical-controller verification, so missing hardware blocks M4 onward

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** PLAN L101 ("If hardware is unavailable, keep this gate open"), L105 (M4 depends on M3), L131.
- **Problem:** The plan's own rule means that without four physical gamepads, the race rules, Classic AI, Roadstormers and everything after them cannot formally start.
- **Correction:** Split M3 into two gates.
  - **M3a:** the rendering, performance and budget freeze. Bulk content and M4 depend on it.
  - **M3b:** physical-device input verification. M5 acceptance and release depend on it.
- **Affected milestones:** M3–M5.
- **Verification:** the updated dependency graph.

### F15. Course lengths and lap targets do not add up, which undermines the "standard three-lap" reward rule

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L57 ("per-course tuning to keep races around three to five minutes"), L107–L114 (3.5–4.3 km; 60–95 s laps), L174 ("standard three-lap" rewards).
- **Problem:**
  - A 60 s lap of 3.5 km averages 210 km/h, at or above several cars' 205–235 km/h top speeds. The 60 s end of the range is unreachable for every listed length.
  - At 95 s, the required averages are 133–163 km/h. That is demanding for courses with 28 m switchback hairpins, such as Alpine at 3.9 km (148 km/h) and Stormpeak at 4.3 km (163 km/h).
  - Realistic laps will probably run 90–110 s, so three laps exceed five minutes.
  - If agents then tune *lap counts* per course, Career races stop being "standard three-lap" races and the credit rule breaks.
- **Correction:**
  - State that course length, not lap count, is tuned to meet race duration, and that Career events are always three laps.
  - Alternatively, apply the lap-ratio scaling to Career rewards too.
  - Restate the lap target as roughly 70–100 s.
- **Affected milestones:** M2.5, M7, M6.6.
- **Verification:** reference-lap times from F1 for every course before the M7 schedule is populated.

### F16. Two working car names are current production model names

- **Severity:** minor
- **Where:** GAME_DESIGN L128, L135 (Comet), L139 (Meridian).
- **Problem:** *Jeep Meridian*, a three-row SUV sold in India since 2022, and *MG Comet EV*, sold in India since 2023, are current production cars. The plan correctly calls the names working names (GAME_DESIGN L128, ASSET_PRODUCTION L22), but renaming after badges, UI cards and thumbnails exist creates avoidable churn.
- **Correction:** Rename these two before the M5 and M7 art. Leave the formal clearance of all names to the existing M9.6 trademark check.

## Feasibility experiments

These are proposals. None was run during this review.

### E1. Drift/grip parity and handling feel (in M1, before M2 course tooling)

- **Inputs:**
  - The M1 motor and the three starter configurations.
  - A graybox loop with five corner classes: a 28 m hairpin, a 50–60 m medium corner, a 100 m sweeper, esses, and a gravel sweeper.
  - The F1 line/speed optimizer, run in grip-only and drift-allowed modes.
  - Two or three people on keyboard and gamepad.
- **Cost limit:** at most 3 agent-days, 4 CPU-hours of parameter sweeps, and 2 hours of total human time.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - For each starter, the optimized drift and grip times are within ±2% per corner class and ±1% per lap.
  - The surge produces a measurable but bounded gain at each qualifying exit.
  - With default assists, 50 scripted routine drifts produce no spins.
  - At least two of three testers sustain a 12–40° drift on the sweeper in 80% or more of attempts after 10 minutes, and rate control at least 4 out of 5.
- **Fail:** retune the drift model before any course work.
- **Resolves:** the drift model, the surge magnitude and the parity metric (F4, F7).

### E2. Training bridge and learning pilot (immediately after M4, in parallel with M5 preparation)

- **Inputs:**
  - Pinned headless Godot (`--headless --fixed-fps 60`).
  - A two-lane graybox segment.
  - Classic AI emitting `TacticalIntent` (F3).
  - The local bridge and pinned SB3 PPO.
  - Two scenario families: passing a slower car on a wide straight, and a two-lane corner approach.
- **Steps:**
  1. Tests for exact tick advancement and reset completeness.
  2. Throughput at 1, 4 and N processes.
  3. Behavioral cloning on about 50k Classic intent samples.
  4. PPO starting from the BC policy with three seeds. Use a low initial action-noise setting and optionally an early KL penalty toward the BC policy. *Hypothesis:* SB3's default exploration noise can wipe out the cloned behavior in the first updates.
- **Cost limit:** at most 24 wall-clock hours on one reference workstation, at most 5M decision steps, and no paid compute.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - Aggregate throughput of at least 300 decision steps per second (about 1M per hour).
  - Resets are exact within a process.
  - At least two of three seeds improve pass success over BC by 15 percentage points or more, with heavy-contact rate no more than 10% above Classic.
  - Surge differs meaningfully from Classic in at least 25% of 200 or more matched windows.
- **Fail:** simplify the observations and scenarios, or try a gradient-free method (see Optional improvements). If it still fails at the cap, write a decision record for the owner.
- **Resolves:** whether learned Roadstormers fit local compute, and the total training budget (F2).

### E3. Four-view rendering budget on reference hardware (front-loads M0.6 and M3.5)

- **Inputs:**
  - A synthetic 600 m course chunk generated at the planned per-view budgets (TECHNICAL_DESIGN L133), with MultiMesh foliage, one shadowed directional light and fog.
  - Eight low-poly cars.
  - One to four SubViewports at 1080p.
  - Both the Compatibility and Mobile renderers, on an M1 with 8 GB and a GTX 1650-class PC.
- **Cost limit:** at most 2 agent-days and about 2 hours per machine.
- **Pass criteria:** p95 ≤ 16.7 ms, p99 ≤ 25 ms, no spike above 50 ms after warm-up, and resident memory ≤ 1.5 GB, with one view and with four.
- **Fail:** find the triangle, shadow and draw-call scaling that does pass.
- **Resolves:** the renderer choice and the per-view art budgets *before* the M2.4 generators are written.

### E4. LLM content "look and listen" spike (before bulk M2.4 kits and M5.4 audio)

- **Inputs:** using only original generators, produce one parametric car, one landmark (for example, a cliff tunnel mouth opening onto the sea), a 300 m scenic road segment, one 60–90 s race theme, and one engine family.
- **Cost limit:** at most 3 agent-days, with no external assets or services.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - Blind rubric review by at least two independent reviewers, including at least one human listener for audio, averages 4 out of 5 or better on silhouette at split-screen size, palette and road-edge readability (including a color-vision simulation), loop seamlessness, timbre, and musical interest.
  - Three feedback iterations show measurable improvement.
  - Loudness and true-peak gates pass.
- **Fail:** admit an existing synthesis engine or scripted modeling tool as development infrastructure.
- **Resolves:** whether the from-scratch toolchains can reach "appealing", and how much revision they need.

### E5. Runtime AI cost microbenchmark (at the start of M4)

- **Inputs, all run in an exported release build on the M1 and x86 reference CPUs:**
  - A typed GDScript MLP forward pass over a `PackedFloat32Array`, at about 3k and about 10k parameters.
  - Observation assembly for 7 nearby cars and 16 look-ahead samples.
  - One Classic tactical update.
  - A full 8-car motor-and-contact tick.
- **Cost limit:** at most 1 agent-day.
- **Pass criteria:** both rivals' inference plus observation assembly ≤ 0.5 ms at p99 per decision update, and the full staggered 8-car tick ≤ 4 ms at p99.
- **Resolves:** the parameter cap and the staggering scheme (F13).

## Cross-document consistency

The following contradictions or missing shared contracts could lead agents to build incompatible systems:

1. **Reference pace** (F1). GAME_DESIGN uses a "documented reference driver", PLAN uses "reference player paces", and OPPONENT_AI uses a "matched Classic driver". No document owns the definition, and TECHNICAL_DESIGN's Track and Car definitions (L80–L81) have no field for reference-lap data.
2. **AI layer contract** (F3). PLAN M4.2 says Classic AI acts "through the player command interface", while OPPONENT_AI defines the intent, safety and follower hierarchy, and TECHNICAL_DESIGN's ownership table omits that hierarchy. Difficulty is required by PLAN M4B.5 but absent from OPPONENT_AI. GAME_DESIGN's reaction delays do not fit the 5 Hz tactical rate.
3. **Retraining trigger vs tuning milestones** (F2). OPPONENT_AI L51 invalidates training on any handling revision, but PLAN schedules handling changes in M5.2, M7 and M8.1 after training.
4. **Owner of cup difficulty.** TECHNICAL_DESIGN L82 puts "AI roster/difficulty/families" in the Event definition, but GAME_DESIGN L170–L172 fixes difficulty "when a cup starts" as a player choice. Recommendation: the Event holds the schedule and default AI families, and the chosen difficulty and Classic Only preference live in the saved cup state.
5. **Career rewards in multiplayer** (F8). PLAN M6.3's "career rewards scoped to the selected host profile" contradicts GAME_DESIGN L71–L72.
6. **Arcade Rush opponents.** GAME_DESIGN L74's "No AI collisions" leaves it unclear whether AI cars are present at all. The mode is single-player, so the simplest reading is that there are no opponents; state this.
7. **Time Attack medals.** "A stock balanced starter" (GAME_DESIGN L81) should name the car, presumably Comet. It should also say whether other cars and assisted runs earn medals or only records.
8. **Rights coverage** (F6). The "unknown legal origin blocks shipping" rule (ASSET_PRODUCTION L119) covers the asset manifest only, while PLAN L21–L22 promises Unlicense/CC0 rights and LLM authorship for code as well.
9. **README license summary.** The baseline README says CC0 covers "original art and audio", while ASSET_PRODUCTION L7 and L13 also dedicate model weights and authored datasets under CC0. They do not conflict, but the summary is incomplete.

## Optional improvements

These are not required fixes. Items that would change the requested scope are labeled **SCOPE CHANGE**.

- **Use Godot's built-in QOA compression for audio.** `AudioStreamWAV` supports `FORMAT_QOA` (verified). Importing 48 kHz WAV masters with QOA compression may remove the need for a separate encoder and its license record, and keeps loop points sample-accurate. Measure decoder cost against Ogg first.
- **Allow an existing synthesis engine** as development infrastructure, with its license recorded, if E4 shows that from-scratch oscillator code limits timbre quality. Recipes and scores stay original, and the rendered outputs are still dedicated under CC0. This follows the project's own rule that existing tools are allowed.
- **Allow a gradient-free fallback for Roadstormers.** If PPO fails E2, the policy is small enough (under 10k parameters) for evolution strategies or CMA-ES over the MLP weights. These parallelize across headless episodes without per-step bridge timing. The result is still a learned policy, not a relabeled script, so this changes method, not scope.
- **Add an early look-development check in M2.** Run one car plus one Riviera vignette through a four-way split capture to test art direction together with the performance budget.
- **Consider parameter sharing across learner cars** in later curriculum stages to raise sample efficiency. This relaxes the "single-agent" baseline in OPPONENT_AI L47, so the non-stationarity risk must be evaluated.
- **SCOPE CHANGE (not recommended now):** decide in advance who chooses, and how cups and unlocks rebalance, if the M7.1 second-course proof shows that six finished courses are not achievable at the intended quality (for example, four at 1.0 and two after release). Recording the decision path now avoids an ad hoc cut later. The plan already forbids silently removing scope (PLAN L229).
- **Testing cost.** The release matrix (T11: 100 seeds × 6 tracks × 3 difficulties = 1,800 races) looks affordable in headless fixed-FPS runs. *Hypothesis:* on the order of 10–20 CPU-hours at the plan's 4 ms simulation budget. The disproportionate cost risk is the retraining loop (F2), not the test matrix.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- **The hierarchical AI** (observation → tactical intent → shared safety layer → shared follower → ordinary motor). It keeps learning small, makes fairness enforceable in one place, and lets learned and Classic AI be compared like for like.
- **One shared fixed-tick simulation** in which presentation, audio and scenery cannot change results, with typed events and explicit teardown. This is the right basis for four players, replays and tests.
- **A custom arcade motor instead of `VehicleBody3D`.** Godot's own documentation says that class "has known issues and isn't designed to provide realistic 3D vehicle physics", and arcade drifting needs authored behavior.
- **A custom pairwise contact resolver** working from a shared tick-start snapshot, with car-to-car physics masked out. This avoids order-dependent shoves and launches.
- **Ordered swept gates as the authority for laps**, kept separate from physical distance and nearest-point projection. This is the correct protection against overpass and shortcut errors.
- **A single track source** compiled into render geometry, collision, AI lines, gates, anchors and the minimap, plus validators that must reject broken fixtures.
- **Honest determinism:** ghosts from sampled transforms rather than input replay, tolerance-based physics regressions, and no claim of cross-platform determinism.
- **Event-instance reward transactions with atomic, backed-up saves** that never invent purchases.
- **A shared priority-weighted audio mix** instead of four positional listener mixes, which avoids doubled 3D audio across viewports.
- **A precise license posture.** It avoids calling the executable "wholly public domain", accepts only explicit item-level CC0 for exceptions, lets unknown legal origin block shipping, and forbids invented model metadata.
- **Gates that cannot be faked:** a Classic fallback that does not count as working Roadstormers, the rule not to expand content to hide weak driving, and the separation of automated checks from experience review.
- **Offline-first design** with no runtime LLM, model download or telemetry, plus loaner cars for multiplayer exhibition so new groups can play fairly at once.
- **Deadline-disabled mechanical completion tests**, measured separately from production deadline DNFs. This removes the temptation to add hidden catch-up.
