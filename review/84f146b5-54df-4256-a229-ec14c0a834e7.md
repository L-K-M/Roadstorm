## Ranked findings

### 1. Major — No pre-agreed contingency if the Roadstormer learning gate cannot be met
- **Where:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` intro ("If learning does not meet its evaluation gate, continue the training/design milestone rather than relabeling a scripted personality as learned AI"); `PLAN.md:12` (two learned Roadstormers are a 1.0 product commitment); `PLAN.md:115-127` (M4B); `PLAN.md:170-182` (M8.7 gate).
- **Problem:** The plan hard-requires shipped learned rivals and forbids relabeling scripted AI, but defines no decision rule for the case where M4B/M8.7 cannot be met within any reasonable compute budget. The only sanctioned responses are "keep the gate open" or an unrecorded scope change, and PLAN's own rule ("do not silently remove a requested feature to mark a milestone complete", PLAN.md:229 area) would then be violated under schedule pressure.
- **Consequence:** If PPO on the Godot bridge plateaus (throughput, reward exploit, or the ≥25% behavioral-distinction threshold in OPPONENT_AI.md:71 proves unreachable), the project either slips M5–M9 indefinitely or de-scopes ad hoc, and concurrent agents receive conflicting instructions.
- **Correction:** Record a decision rule now: the learned-rival gate stays open until met; any fallback to a Classic-Only 1.0 is an explicit owner scope change that must update PLAN.md:12, the mode tables, and OPPONENT_AI.md together (the plan already requires cross-document updates for shared counts). Keep the safe Classic fallback strictly as fault handling, as the plan states.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M4B, M5.7, M7.7, M8.7. Verification: a decision record in the decision log at M4B start; M4B.7 held-out evaluation and M8.7 full gate results against the OPPONENT_AI.md:63-75 table.

### 2. Major — Four-camera 60 FPS at 1080p on GTX 1650-class hardware is the highest technical risk and is (correctly) gated, but the aggregate budget assumes per-view culling that is not yet evidenced
- **Where:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:126-133` (reference profiles; p95 ≤16.7 ms / p99 ≤25 ms; ≤350k tris/250 calls single view, ≤900k/650 aggregated across four views); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:104-106` (one `World3D`, four `SubViewport`s); `PLAN.md:91-101` (M3), `PLAN.md:216-229` (risk table).
- **Problem:** In a shared `World3D` with four viewports, Godot culls per viewport, but the 900k/650 aggregate allowance (vs 4× single-view = 1400k/1000) silently assumes culling efficiency of ~36% across quadrants. On a GTX 1650-class GPU with Compatibility rendering, four 1080p viewports with overlapping scenery could exceed the draw-call budget. This is a hypothesis, not a confirmed defect — the plan sequences M3 as a hard gate before bulk content and mandates a renderer comparison (M0.6; TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:9), which is exactly right.
- **Consequence:** If M3 measurements fail on the reference machine, late discovery would force scenery/handling compromises across all six courses; the plan mitigates this, but the fallback levers are not named in advance.
- **Correction:** Keep M3 as a blocking gate (do not weaken it). Predefine the fallback order now: (a) choose Mobile vs Compatibility from M0.6 measurements, (b) default 4-player sessions to the 70% 3D-resolution preset, (c) trim per-view scenery LOD/instancing, (d) only then reduce the target. Record the chosen renderer and budget in the frozen rendering budget (M3.5).
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M3.4-M3.5, M7 (per-course recheck, PLAN.md:168), M8.3, T14. Verification: worst four-camera scene frame-time distributions on the recorded reference machine, per Experiment E1 below.

### 3. Minor — Lap-time targets, course lengths, and top-speed caps are jointly infeasible at the low end of the range
- **Where:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:35` (stock top speeds 205–235 km/h = 57–65 m/s), `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:107-114` (course lengths 3.5–4.3 km; "Target 60–95-second laps"; races "around three to five minutes" at default three laps, :57).
- **Problem (arithmetic, confirmed):** 4.3 km in 60 s requires a 71.7 m/s average, above the 65 m/s maximum top speed; even 3.5 km in 60 s requires 58.3 m/s average through corners, which is not credible. At a realistic 45–55 m/s average, the 4.3 km Stormpeak laps land near 78–96 s, so three laps = 3.9–4.8 min — inside the 3–5 min window only at its top. The 60 s lower bound is reachable, if at all, only on the shortest course.
- **Consequence:** Implementation agents setting per-course lap targets, Arcade Rush allowances (which derive from measured clean laps, GAME_DESIGN.md:88-89), or medal times from the 60 s end will produce unreachable targets and churn in M6/M7 tuning.
- **Correction:** State that the 60 s bound applies only to the shortest courses; set per-course lap targets from the measured reference pace in M2 (the doc already says "after measurement and adjustment"), and reconcile the 3–5 min race window by allowing per-course default laps (e.g., 3 laps short courses, fewer on Stormpeak) or by trimming the longest routes. Keep "course length is provisional" as the governing rule.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M2.5/M2 acceptance timing, M6.1, M7.5. Verification: documented per-course reference lap times from the seeded harness before Arcade Rush allowances and medals are authored.

### 4. Minor — Grid ordering outside Career is an unspecified essential rule
- **Where:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:59` (only Career is specified: "Rotate the player's starting row across career events"; `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:170` fixes the cup seed at cup start). The Event contract table in `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` ("Content contracts") lists no grid-order field.
- **Problem:** For Quick Race, Time Attack-adjacent events, Arcade Rush, and local exhibition, nothing says how the eight grid slots are ordered (seeded shuffle? join order? fixed AI order with the player row rotated?). Two agents implementing M4.1 vs M6.1 could build incompatible grid logic and replay/seed semantics.
- **Consequence:** Non-reproducible Quick Race grids, divergent tie/seed handling, and save/replay mismatches discovered only in M8 testing.
- **Correction:** Add one rule to the Event contract: grid order is a seeded permutation recorded in the event instance (same pattern as the Career roster fix), with the documented player-row rotation rule; Quick Race exposes/derives the seed. One sentence plus a contract-table field.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M4.1, M6.1, T05/T06. Verification: a constructed scenario that rebuilds the exact grid from a recorded seed, exact-check in the test matrix.

### 5. Minor — The learned policy's essential learning rules (observation schema, reward coefficients) are deliberately deferred, so OPPONENT_AI.md alone does not fully specify the training problem
- **Where:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md:25` ("Define and version the exact feature list before training"), :47 (reward shape described, weights not given); `PLAN.md:116` (M4B.1 freezes the contract).
- **Problem:** This is a legitimate staging decision, and the review question "can an agent build without inventing essential rules" is answered *yes* for the game and *not yet* for the trainer — but only if the frozen contract is actually reviewed against the gates table before training starts, which the docs imply but do not require.
- **Consequence:** If the observation list or reward weights are pinned without checking them against the behavioral-distinction and safety-intervention gates (OPPONENT_AI.md:71,74), the M4B gate can become unpassable for reasons unrelated to learning skill.
- **Correction:** Add an explicit requirement to M4B.1: the frozen observation/intent contract and reward revision must be a reviewed document, cross-checked line-by-line against the gates table (lines 63-75) and the "predefine categories before seeing results" rule, before M4B.3 begins.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M4B.1-M4B.3. Verification: contract document + schema-validation tests + the ≥10,000-observation parity corpus (OPPONENT_AI.md:65).

### 6. Minor — No owned task for name/trademark clearance, and no required listening pass over shipped music
- **Where:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md:22` (names "remain working names until appropriately checked"; "not a completed trademark search"), `PLAN.md:209` (M9.6 only says confirm the title "if those have changed"); `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` music workflow ("If tools cannot hear the result, report that limit"); `PLAN.md:182` (M8 acceptance explicitly permits release with no human playtest, reported as a limitation).
- **Problem:** (a) Public-domain dedication is correctly stated to not clear trademarks, but no milestone task owns the actual clearance check for "Roadstorm Racer!" and the twelve car names before public release. (b) Music quality is a stated product quality ("original synth music"), yet nothing requires anyone with working ears to listen to all six race themes before M8 closes; LLM meter checks are explicitly not proof of musical quality.
- **Consequence:** (a) A name collision discovered after release forces a rebrand across assets, strings, and marketing — cheap now, expensive later. (b) The game can ship with unlistened-to music if no human ever plays.
- **Correction:** (a) Add an explicit name-clearance task (search + owner decision + rename procedure) to M9.6, keeping "working title" labeling until cleared. (b) Add a recorded listening review of every shipped theme and key effect set to M5.4/M8.2 — listening is playtest feedback, which the plan already permits humans to give, so this does not alter the LLM-production scope. Both are one-task additions, not scope changes.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M5.4, M8.2, M9.6, T13/T15. Verification: clearance decision record; per-track review notes in the manifest `Review` field.

## Feasibility experiments (proposals, not run during this review)

1. **E1 — Four-camera rendering stress (resolves Finding 2).** Inputs: M2 Riviera graybox with representative scenery, 8 scripted cars, four SubViewports at 1920×1080, recorded reference machine (M1 8 GB; GTX 1650-class). Cost limit: one machine-day. Pass/fail: p95 ≤16.7 ms, p99 ≤25 ms, ≤900k tris/650 calls aggregated, per TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:130-133, measured on both Compatibility and Mobile renderers. Decision: lock the renderer and 4-player default resolution preset; if failed, apply the predefined fallback order before any M7 content.
2. **E2 — Training-bridge throughput pilot (de-risks Finding 1).** Inputs: headless Godot bridge per OPPONENT_AI.md:39-41, 10,000–100,000 decision steps (one step = twelve 60 Hz ticks), local hardware only. Cost limit: pilot capped at a fixed wall time and step count, whichever first (OPPONENT_AI.md:45). Pass/fail: measured steps/sec supports a learning curve review within the cap, with exact tick-advance and full-reset tests passing. Decision: approve, resize, or simplify the training budget before any long PPO run.
3. **E3 — Reward-exploit probe (de-risks Finding 1).** Inputs: BC-initialized policy plus scripted exploit scenarios (gate skipping, wall riding, donuts, weaving, blocking) with per-component reward logging (OPPONENT_AI.md:47). Cost limit: one training day on the pilot budget. Pass/fail: no exploit yields positive reward; safety interventions within OPPONENT_AI.md:74 thresholds. Decision: finalize reward weights/curriculum before full PPO.
4. **E4 — Drift/grip parity bench (M1 gate).** Inputs: benchmark sweeper, one starter car, fixed upgrade preset; clean drift line vs clean grip line, repeated runs at 30/60/120 rendered FPS. Cost limit: hours. Pass/fail: elapsed times within 5% (PLAN.md:76) and fixed-tick results within tolerance across rendered FPS. Decision: handling tuning is locked before M2 course authoring; a fail blocks content, per the risk table.
5. **E5 — Behavioral-distinction dry run (de-risks Finding 1/5).** Inputs: matched Classic baseline vs BC-initialized Surge/Switch candidates, ≥200 matched opportunity windows per pair across the six scenario families (OPPONENT_AI.md:71). Cost limit: bounded by E2 throughput. Pass/fail: ≥25% meaningful (not raw-numeric) differences per pair, categories predefined. Decision: confirms the observation features carry style signal before expensive RL; a fail sends the team back to feature design, not to more compute.

## Strong decisions to preserve

1. **Simulation/presentation separation with fixed ticks, seeded tests, and an honest non-determinism caveat** (TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:64-70, "do not promise cross-platform deterministic Godot physics"): this is what makes fairness, replays, and the training bridge possible at all. Keep it.
2. **Decision gates M1/M3/M4B/M5 before bulk content, plus "do not replace a failed gate with a larger batch of generated content"** (PLAN.md:47-49, 228): exactly the right defense against the two biggest risks. Keep the gates blocking.
3. **The AI fairness contract** — same `DriverCommand` structure, same physical limits, explicit banned mechanics (no catch-up, teleport, secret grip), predefined intervention categories, ≥3 training seeds, held-out courses, and the anti-inert-copy ≥25% behavioral-distinction threshold (OPPONENT_AI.md:9,25,63-75): an unusually rigorous learned-AI spec; preserve it verbatim through M4B/M8.
4. **Honest licensing posture** (ASSET_PRODUCTION.md:5-24): Unlicense for code / CC0 for assets / engine notices separate, no "whole binary is public domain" claim, no fabricated originality guarantees, unknown legal origin blocks shipping, original font instead of a silent default, trademark caveat stated. Verified against live primary sources. Preserve.
5. **Economy and progression design**: internally exact math, lap-scaled rewards against farming, once-per-event transactions, no credit-gated cars, loaners for local play — consistent across GAME_DESIGN.md and PLAN.md. Preserve.
6. **Explicit honesty about verification limits** ("an untested platform does not become verified through a successful export", PLAN.md:63; "metrics alone cannot close this milestone", PLAN.md:76; "if there has been no independent human playtest, report that limitation", PLAN.md:182): keep this culture; it is what makes the acceptance gates trustworthy.
