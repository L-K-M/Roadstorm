## Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.** The architecture, milestone ordering, and honesty about uncertainty are strong, and an unfamiliar agent could start M0-M2 productively from these documents. What blocks "as written" is a set of contract and gate clarifications: the learned-Roadstormer feature is a hard launch requirement on the critical path with no bounded exit, the tactical interface between the Classic planner, safety layer, follower, and learned policy is not fixed, several acceptance metrics are ambiguous or arithmetically inconsistent, and the public-domain premise for agent-authored code needs a provider-terms rule. None of these require redesigning the game; all are fixable before the affected milestones. Residual uncertainty remains in the two areas the plan itself gates earliest - learned-rival behaviour and four-camera cost - and those should be treated as experiments, not assumptions.

## Ranked findings

### 1. A required flagship feature has no bounded go/no-go or descope decision

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 5 ("Roadstormers are a required part of the updated launch scope... continue the training/design milestone rather than relabeling a scripted personality as learned AI"); `PLAN.md` line 12 (two Roadstormers by default), lines 117-127 (M4B), line 131 (M5 depends on M4B), lines 222-223 (risk rows "keep the gate open").
- **Problem:** The only prescribed response to failing the learned-rival evaluation gate is to keep iterating. There is no pre-registered pass threshold for the pilot, no maximum extension budget, and no owner decision path if the gate still fails. Because M4B gates M5, M7 retraining, and M8 closure, an unresolved M4B stalls the entire launch; the alternative the document forbids (relabelling scripted personalities as learned) is the obvious pressure relief.
- **Failure example:** After the M4B.2 throughput pilot, BC+PPO misses the behavioural-distinction or pace thresholds. Curriculum and reward iterations continue for several cycles; M5 content work waits, or the team quietly ships Classic personalities and changes their names, which violates the stated requirement and the review's reason for existing.
- **Correction:** At M4B.2, define a bounded pilot with quantitative pass criteria and a hard step/wall-clock cap. Pre-agree that a second failure triggers an explicit owner scope decision: one fixed-budget retrain, or deferral of Roadstormers to a post-launch update with Classic personalities filling the slots. Record the decision in `docs/STATUS.md`. The deferral option changes requested scope and must be labelled as such.
- **Milestones / verification:** M4B, M5, M7, M8. Verify with the pilot learning curves, held-out metrics, and a signed decision record.

### 2. The shared AI tactical contract is described in prose but never versioned

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 19-25 (observation builder, `TacticalIntent`, safety layer, follower; "Define and version the exact feature list before training"), line 27 (model), line 57 (model card); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 56 (`DriverCommand`), lines 78-86 (content contracts: AI profile and Model rows only); `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 99 ("legal lane occupancy").
- **Problem:** Four components - Classic planner, safety layer, path follower, and learned policy - must agree on one interface, but the documents specify it only narratively. `TacticalIntent`'s fields (lane/offset, target pace, follow/pass commitment, drift preference) have no types, units, ranges, defaults, or coordinate frame. The observation vector is explicitly deferred to M4B.1. There is no lane model (lane count, width, how occupancy is computed), and no contract row for the observation/action schema or the safety layer's outputs. `PLAN.md` line 24 says shared counts/rules must change together, but nothing currently freezes them, contrary to `PLAN.md` line 210 ("changes affecting a shared contract land before dependent implementation").
- **Failure example:** The Classic-AI agent implements lane offset in metres with a discrete three-lane enum; the inference agent implements a continuous normalized [-1,1] lateral offset plus a history stack. Parity tests and safety-intervention accounting compare incompatible quantities, so the "same controller" fairness guarantee becomes untestable and M4B.7 cannot produce a meaningful comparison.
- **Correction:** Before M4B.1 (and before M4.2), add an integration-owned, versioned interface schema covering: observation features (name, dtype, shape, normalization constants, source), intent/action fields (name, unit, range, default, frame), lane and occupancy model, macro-step cadence, and invalid/sentinel values. Add matching rows to `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`'s content contracts and a schema-lint plus parity harness. Dependent work waits for the frozen revision.
- **Milestones / verification:** M4.2, M4B.1, M4B.4. Verify with schema validation over constructed edge cases and the 10,000-observation parity corpus.

### 3. Lap-time targets contradict course lengths and top speeds

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 35 (top speed 205-235 km/h), lines 107-112 (course lengths 3.5-4.3 km), line 114 ("Target 60-95-second laps"), line 57 (races "around three to five minutes").
- **Problem:** A 60 s lap of 4.3 km requires a 258 km/h average; 4.1 km requires 246 km/h. Both exceed the 235 km/h stock ceiling even at flat-out with zero cornering, and the upgrade system caps top speed at +3% (about 242 km/h). A 3.9 km lap needs roughly a 234 km/h average, i.e. effectively no slow corners. So the 60 s lower bound is physically unreachable for Alpine Switchback, Canyon Voltage, and Stormpeak Finale. Realistic laps with hairpins and tight sequences will land above 95 s, which also breaks the three-to-five-minute race commitment for three laps.
- **Failure example:** During M2.5, a clean reference lap of the Riviera graybox measures 105 s. The 60-95 s target then forces either an unrequested handling retune or silent course shortening, and M2 acceptance fails after authoring has begun.
- **Correction:** Derive per-course lap targets from measured length divided by a stated planned average speed, or shorten courses / raise speed targets, and recompute the race-duration commitment from those numbers. Do this before M2.5 route authoring so the authored geometry and the target pacing agree.
- **Milestones / verification:** M2.5, M7.5, M8.1. Verify with timed reference runs on the Riviera graybox and a revised course table.

### 4. The four-camera rendering budget is not measurable as written

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 133 ("<=350k visible triangles/250 draw calls in one view; <=900k triangles/650 draw calls aggregated across four views"), line 139 (measurement guidance); `PLAN.md` M3.5 (line 99) and M3 acceptance (line 101).
- **Problem:** Godot submits geometry per active SubViewport camera, so four views multiply submission work unless culling/LOD differ per view. "Aggregated" is undefined: summed submissions, unique geometry, or per-view average give materially different results. There is also no CPU render-submission or render-thread budget for four viewports (the 4 ms simulation budget excludes rendering), and the numeric row does not encode the worst-case four-simultaneous-camera scene that M3.5 claims to measure.
- **Failure example:** M3 passes because each viewport measures 300 draw calls and 300k triangles and the reviewer averages per view (sum 1200 calls/1.2M), while the GTX 1650-class reference machine drops below 60 FPS on CPU submission; alternatively the gate fails on one camera angle after art is complete, forcing expensive cuts.
- **Correction:** Define per-frame metrics explicitly - summed draw calls and triangles across all active SubViewports, plus CPU submit and render-thread milliseconds - and require the representative stress scene rendered through four simultaneous cameras on the recorded reference hardware at default settings. Freeze these definitions in M3.5 before bulk art.
- **Milestones / verification:** M0.6, M3.5, M8.3. Verify with an instrumented capture script that reports exactly these metric names.

### 5. The learned-rival pace gate is too loose and directionally ambiguous

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 70 ("Clean mixed-course lap pace within approximately 10% of the matched Classic driver"); contrast `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 93-95 (Classic pace as 85-103% of a reference).
- **Problem:** "Within approximately 10%" does not state faster or slower, and 10% of a 60-95 s lap is 6-9.5 seconds. A Surge rival that far off cannot sustain pressure; a Switch rival that far off cannot contest corner exits. The gate also uses clean lap pace, which does not test the traffic situations where the style claim is made.
- **Failure example:** Evaluation reports a learned rival at -9% lap pace and marks the gate passed; in a race it loses more than five seconds per lap and never contests a pass, so "pressure-oriented" is unsupported despite a green checkbox.
- **Correction:** Replace with a per-course lap-time ratio band versus a matched Classic at the same difficulty (for example -1% to +3%), require the measurement in traffic scenarios, state the direction convention, and treat a result outside +/-3% as a retune trigger rather than a pass.
- **Milestones / verification:** M4B.5, M4B.7, M7.7, M8.7. Verify with matched-pair race telemetry on identical cars, seeds, and difficulty.

### 6. Public-domain dedication for agent-authored code ignores coding-provider terms

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` line 1 (public-domain goal), line 22 (licensing can dedicate only rights the contributor holds; AI generation alone establishes neither copyrightability nor freedom from claims), lines 28-30 (provider records and contribution dedication), line 103 (provider-terms rule scoped to "remote image/audio/model generation"), lines 112-119 (manifest Origin/Rights), line 123 (release gate); `PLAN.md` line 22; `README.md` license section.
- **Problem:** The manifest records provider/model/date, but the external-material policy's provider-terms requirement is scoped to remote image/audio/model generation. Code-writing agent providers are not covered, yet the contribution rule assumes a contributor can accept the Unlicense/CC0 dedication "for rights the contributor controls." If a coding agent's terms do not grant the user rights to copy, redistribute, and dedicate its output, the Unlicense claim on that code is unsupported, and there is no intake rule to catch it before release.
- **Failure example:** The M8.6 audit finds that a core simulator module was produced under provider terms that retain distribution rights or prohibit public-domain dedication. Replacing it late invalidates reviews, retraining, and already-generated content dependencies.
- **Correction:** Extend the provenance manifest's Origin/Rights fields to every agent-authored code artifact (provider, account/plan, date, applicable terms, redistribution and dedication permissions), add an intake rule that incompatible terms block commit (mirroring line 119's "unknown legal origin blocks shipping"), and record provider terms in M0.3 before production starts.
- **Milestones / verification:** M0.3, intake for M2/M4/M7, M8.6. Verify with a manifest-validation test over a sample of authored files.

### 7. Contact-resolver sequencing and mutable-state hand-off are underspecified

- **Severity:** major.
- **Location:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` lines 62-70 (custom motor, masks, tick-start snapshot, stable order, recheck world, avoid double response); `PLAN.md` M1.4 and M4.4.
- **Problem:** Ownership is clear, but the per-tick sequence and state hand-off are not: who integrates velocity, when tick-start snapshots are captured, how positional correction interacts with `CharacterBody3D.move_and_slide`, what happens when correction reaches its cap, and how contact/immunity/progress state is updated afterwards. Different agents will choose different orderings, and ordering changes outcomes in exactly the cases M1.4/M4.4 gate: tunneling above 1 m/tick, vertical launches, ramming, and teleporting catch-up.
- **Failure example:** One agent moves all bodies then resolves pairs (endpoint overlaps); another resolves pairs before world sweeps. In the same max-speed rear-end fixture, the first lets a car pass through a barrier and the second launches the rear-ended car. T04 cannot distinguish a correct implementation from a lucky one.
- **Correction:** Add a short normative sequence to `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`: snapshot all body states, integrate each body, resolve world sweeps, resolve pairwise swept contacts from the snapshot, apply bounded mass-weighted response and capped positional correction, re-resolve world collision, then update contact/immunity/progress state. Assign each step an owner. Add constructed fixtures: head-on at maximum speed, rear-end closure greater than 1 m/tick, three-car pileup, contact at a bridge edge, and correction-cap saturation.
- **Milestones / verification:** M1.4, M4.4, T04, T10. Verify with deterministic contact fixtures and asserted impulse bounds.

### 8. The "meaningful difference" rubric for learned styles is not operationalized

- **Severity:** minor.
- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 71 (>=25% "meaningful differences"), lines 72 and 77 (readable styles, full-clip inspection), line 79 (predefine categories and thresholds).
- **Problem:** Categories and intervention thresholds are pre-registered, but "meaningful" has no definition, rater protocol, or agreement threshold. Excluding raw numeric differences is good; the replacement is whatever reviewers decide after seeing results.
- **Failure example:** Evaluators count any lane-choice flip as meaningful and pass a policy that oscillates between offsets without changing pass outcomes; or two reviewers disagree and the gate stalls without a resolution rule.
- **Correction:** Pre-register a blinded pairwise clip/choice protocol (at least two independent reviewers, inter-rater agreement, category definitions, and the flip-rate computation) before M4B.7, and report raw counts alongside the rubric verdict.
- **Milestones / verification:** M4B.7, M8.7. Verify with the rubric document and an agreement statistic.

### 9. The course design contract is not enforced by the track validator

- **Severity:** minor.
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 116-120 (required sweepers, turning sequences, acceleration straight, width transitions, climb/descent, crest/compression); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` lines 90-92 (validator list) and T09; `PLAN.md` M2.3.
- **Problem:** The technical validator enumerates curvature, stopping distance, clearance, self-intersections, grid space, barriers, and recovery, but not rhythm/feature presence. An agent following the validator can ship a course that passes T09 while violating the design contract, or reject a valid course that satisfies the contract but trips a geometric warning.
- **Failure example:** The Alpine graybox validates geometrically with only one broad sweeper; M2 acceptance passes on the enumerated checks and the missing required feature surfaces during playtest.
- **Correction:** Add a machine-checkable design checklist (or an explicit manual sign-off row in T09) mapping each contract item to evidence, and validate it in M2.3 before route authoring is declared done.
- **Milestones / verification:** M2.3, M7.6, T09. Verify with a deliberately broken fixture that fails the checklist.

### 10. Minor rule ambiguities that invite exploits or inconsistent implementations

- **Severity:** minor.
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 31 and 47 (drift suppression on "wall contact", "valid forward motion"), line 61 (tie wording), line 57 with line 174 (humans-only 2-4 car field versus standard eight-car reward rules); `PLAN.md` line 148 ("achievement unlocks").
- **Problem:** Drift reward suppression has no contact-severity threshold and no definition of valid forward motion, so wall-brush farming is possible or surge becomes inconsistent. The ranking sentence says participant ID orders an exact internal tie and also that a tie is shown when displayed times coincide, which are different behaviours. "Achievement unlocks" has no definition anywhere, while the only defined unlock sources are cup awards. Credit treatment for a 2-4 car humans-only Quick Race is unspecified.
- **Failure example:** A player maintains a scrape along a barrier through a corner and receives no surge in one build and full style credit in another; or a results screen shows a shared place while the cup points table silently ordered by participant ID.
- **Correction:** Define contact-severity thresholds and the reward-suppression state machine; rewrite the tie sentence to separate internal ordering from displayed ties; define or remove "achievement unlocks"; state whether reduced-field events pay the standard base and placement rewards.
- **Milestones / verification:** M1.3, M4.1, M6.1/M6.2. Verify with T02 boundary cases and results-screen tests.

## Feasibility experiments

These are proposals for the implementation team, not actions taken during this review.

1. **Lap-time and pace reconciliation.** Inputs: Riviera graybox from M2.5, the three starter cars at stock, and a scripted reference driver. Procedure: run at least 20 clean laps, record average speed, lap time, and minimum corner speeds. Cost limit: one working session, fully automated. Pass: three-lap race duration lands in the intended window with average speed below roughly 85% of top speed. Fail: revise course lengths, top-speed targets, or the duration commitment before art production. Resolves finding 3.
2. **Headless training throughput pilot.** Inputs: M4B.2 bridge, Classic demonstrations, and a bounded curriculum slice. Procedure: benchmark 10,000-100,000 decision steps across 1, 4, and 8 parallel Godot workers on the recorded reference machine; measure steps/second, wall time, memory, and IPC overhead. Cost limit: a pre-agreed wall-clock cap and disk budget, stopping at whichever comes first. Pass: projected full curriculum with at least three seeds fits the agreed budget. Fail: reduce scenario length, batch size, or model size before any compute expansion. Resolves finding 1 and the plan's training-speed risk row.
3. **Matched learned-versus-Classic probe.** Inputs: a minimal BC-only policy, the M4B.2 bridge, and Riviera. Procedure: run 50 paired races against matched Classic profiles at identical difficulty, cars, and seeds; measure lap-time ratio, pass success, contact rate, resets, and safety interventions. Cost limit: bounded pilot compute from experiment 2. Pass: pace within the revised band, no elevated heavy-contact rate, and visibly different choice distributions. Fail: revise observations/reward/curriculum before committing to full PPO, or trigger the scope decision in finding 1. Resolves findings 1 and 5.
4. **Four-camera cost and metric-definition probe.** Inputs: the M3.4 stress scene, eight cars, representative scenery, and the recorded reference machines. Procedure: render one to four simultaneous SubViewports at 1920x1080 with worst-case camera directions; record summed draw calls, triangles, CPU submit time, render-thread time, p95/p99 frame times, and memory. Cost limit: days of engineering time, no art expansion. Pass: 60 FPS with headroom under the definitions agreed in finding 4. Fail: cut scenery/shadows/transparency or reduce 3D resolution scale before locking the renderer. Resolves finding 4 and de-risks the plan's second-largest technical risk.
5. **Deterministic LLM asset regeneration pilot.** Inputs: one car with three LODs, six prop designs, and one music loop, each with a recipe and brief. Procedure: generate twice from clean checkouts with pinned tool versions; compare normalised hashes and run blind silhouette/readability and audio-preview review. Cost limit: a bounded token/time budget and one review round. Pass: byte-identical normalised outputs and reviewer-approved quality at both garage and split-screen sizes. Fail: revise recipes and review loop before bulk content, and reconsider the 50-70 prop inventory or the deterministic-regeneration requirement. Resolves the LLM-production practicality question for M2/M5/M7.

## Optional improvements

These are not required to start and are separate from the ranked fixes.

- Pre-register the evaluation rubrics and intervention categories earlier (supports findings 5 and 8; process only).
- Add the design-contract checker and a short art-direction lock document at M5 (supports findings 3 and 9; process only).
- Add a debug replay viewer for players. This would add scope and is not needed for 1.0; the current diagnostic captures already support development.
- Post-launch candidates already named by the plan (reverse courses, route branches, online multiplayer, cloud LLM opponent) remain scope changes; any decision to add them should be recorded as such.
- Reducing course lengths to hit lap-time targets would change the stated six-course content footprint; label it as a scope/content change if chosen.
- Deferring Roadstormers (finding 1's fallback) changes the requested launch scope and requires explicit owner approval; it is not a silent default.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- One shared fixed-tick simulation with strict separation from cameras, audio, UI, and scenery animation; an audiovisual improvement cannot change a race result. This is the foundation of fair four-player play and repeatable tests.
- The grip-versus-drift parity gate and capped, non-stackable drift reward with anti-farming rules. It protects the accessible-but-competitive handling identity the project is built around.
- Both opponent families use the same command structure, vehicle resources, upgrades, and physical limits, with no position-based catch-up or hidden multipliers. This is the right fairness baseline and should survive any learned-policy tuning.
- Offline-only, small MLP inference implemented in GDScript with numerical parity tests, a disabled training bridge in shipped builds, and a safe Classic fallback. It keeps the modern-AI claim concrete without runtime services or large models.
- Early decision gates at M1, M3, M4B, and M5, with interface freeze before bulk content. This is the plan's best defence against generated-content bloat.
- Conservative licensing posture: original-only baseline, full manifest and provenance, exact engine notices, explicit refusal to claim guaranteed originality, and honest trademark caution. Preserve it even if finding 6 requires extra bookkeeping.
- Honest separation of automated verification from experiential review, including the instruction to report a missing human playtest rather than treating bot results as proof of fun.

## Closing note

The plan is unusually self-aware about its own risks. Most of the findings above are cases where a stated intention is not yet turned into a measurable contract or a bounded decision. Fixing those before the corresponding milestones should leave a buildable, coherent specification.
