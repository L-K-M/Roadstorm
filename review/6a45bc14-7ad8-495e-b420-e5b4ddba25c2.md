## Ranked findings

### 1. The fixed-tick simulator must be proved before the motor and rules depend on it

**Severity — Major.** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:60–72` (Movement and contacts), `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md:37–45` (Training approach), `PLAN.md:65–76,115–127` (M1, M4B).

The training bridge promises exactly twelve gameplay/physics ticks per step and complete reset of hidden state. M1 can currently commit to an engine-callback and movement strategy before this requirement is tested. For example, looping twelve times in a single callback can advance counters while physics queries or engine-owned state update differently. Conversely, running a capped wall-clock loop can produce correct tick counts but inadequate decision throughput for six or more style/seed runs. Godot documents that `move_and_slide()` uses the physics-step delta and modifies body velocity, and that regular engine scheduling limits physics steps per rendered frame. These details constrain the design and leave accelerated stepping to be demonstrated. [CharacterBody3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_characterbody3d.html#class-characterbody3d-method-move-and-slide), [Engine physics scheduling](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_engine.html#class-engine-property-max-physics-steps-per-frame)

Place a two-car tick/reset probe in M0/M1. Give one coordinator ownership of a logical tick and use the same movement, contacts, progress, RNG and timers in interactive and headless runs. Prototype engine-scheduled ticks first and measure them. If accelerating by making more than one logical tick per callback, prove that the chosen query/transform synchronization and explicit-delta movement preserve parity. Contract the bridge around request IDs, clean initial observations, completion or timeout, and fail-closed worker restart. Twelve ticks applies to full steps. Specify terminal-step handling so a car receives no reward after ending an episode. Keep Gymnasium termination/truncation distinct and preserve the terminal observation through any SB3 autoreset. [Gymnasium Env](https://gymnasium.farama.org/api/env/), [SB3 vector environments](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/vec_envs.html)

**Milestones and verification —** M0/M1, then M4B. Run experiment 1, including idle-bridge parity, reset equivalence and contact fixtures. Use the measured end-to-end rate to approve or redesign the pilot before committing to full training.

### 2. On-road movement and valid race progress need one ordering contract

**Severity — Major.** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:41–47,55–65,116–124` (Contact and recovery, Standard race, Course acceptance), `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:60–72,88–96` (Movement and contacts, Track compiler), `PLAN.md:69–76,82–89,107–113` (M1, M2, M4).

Static sweeps, relative car sweeps and ordered gates each have a proposed owner. Their handoff is missing. A predicted trajectory can cross a gate after a barrier should have stopped it, and positional depenetration can carry a car across a gate without driven motion. Ordered gates alone also permit an infield cut that reaches the next gate in order. Bounds on projection help ranking and still leave the legality of that crossing open. Respawn behavior is unspecified when every nearby eligible anchor is occupied or immunity ends during overlap.

Define the tick sequence from one start snapshot through world-clipped swept trajectories, paired contact resolution and bounded world-safe correction. Use the resulting physical motion segments and crossing fractions for timing. Mark reset and correction displacements ineligible for crossings. Give gates/support zones explicit route, height and bounded lateral validity plus a defined rejoin rule for leaving the permitted corridor. Author intentional jumps and overpasses as exceptions with their own eligibility bounds. Specify checkpoint recovery state together with progress, and fall back to an earlier clear anchor or wait when anchors are occupied. Apply reset time loss consistently and specify how immunity ends without an overlap impulse. Pin which movement primitive owns slide and grounding so probe adhesion and engine floor snap cannot fight each other.

**Milestones and verification —** M1/M2 before M4. Exercise a car pushed toward a wall and gate in one tick, three cars in a squeeze, forward crossings after reset, infield cuts that hit the next gate, bridge/road overlap, and blocked anchors. Verify resolved motion, position and race events together.

### 3. Roadstormers need an executable tactical contract and a controlled comparison

**Severity — Major.** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md:15–31,33–51,63–81` (Architecture, Training approach, Acceptance), `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:83–99,149–155` (Opponents, Upgrades), `PLAN.md:115–127,174–180` (M4B, M8).

The observation/action details are intentionally deferred. Assign their design to M4 because Classic demonstrations and the follower depend on them. Clarify the opening statement that both AI families and humans share a low-level controller. The AI path follower is shared by the two AI families, and humans join them at the command and motor boundary. In particular, lane offset, pace, categorical intent and drift preference can form an incompatible tuple. The release actor may also see current positions through derived occupancy while its explicit car inputs are delayed. At 5 Hz a 120 ms Expert delay cannot be represented consistently by rounding it to whole tactical steps. If the follower or safety layer ultimately chooses the passing side, a comparison of raw policy output rewards variation that the player never sees. Conversely two policies can differ often because of pointless waits and still hit the 25% divergence target.

Freeze stable neighbor slots and masks, feature units, observation timestamps, history at reset, and an explicit mapping to legal actions. Start with a small discrete set of maneuver templates compatible with the follower and a teacher label per opportunity. A continuous or hybrid head is viable if the training distribution, decoding and deterministic deployment are defined together. Separate delayed tactical observations from immediate safety for both families and document genuine exceptions such as imminent collision avoidance. Log the Classic proposal and the legal executed intent so imitation labels and guard edits stay distinguishable. Compare Classic, cloned-only, Surge and Switch under identical car, upgrades, difficulty, follower and safety settings. Match opportunities from a common snapshot or controlled scripted traffic. Score realized clean passes, pressure-to-pass and entry-to-exit cutbacks, alongside contact, pace, interventions and completion. Predefine heavy-contact units and exposure, the near-zero allowance, per-style/course reporting, and uncertainty by independent race or scenario seed. An aggregate AI-entry rate can mask individual model failures. Measure budget from observation through decoded action and test legal-action parity near decision boundaries in addition to numerical parity. SB3 documents the need to preserve action postprocessing when deploying a policy. [SB3 export guide](https://stable-baselines3.readthedocs.io/en/master/guide/export.html)

**Milestones and verification —** M4 fixes the interfaces, M4B tests causal style differences and bounded local training, M7/M8 verify generalization. Reserve one whole launch course across training and model selection and include human-driven or reproducibly replayed traffic patterns in held-out tests. Keep the launch gate open if guard repair or scripted fallback accounts for the apparent style.

### 4. Race termination, ties and withdrawals need exact outcomes

**Severity — Major.** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:55–75,157–176,186–194` (Race rules, Career and rewards, Local multiplayer interface), `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:41–58,145–153` (Runtime ownership, Verification matrix), `PLAN.md:91–113,143–154` (M3, M4, M6).

Both production finish deadlines start at the first finish. In a two-human race where both stop, no deadline begins. The mechanical harness also disables those deadlines without a stated watchdog result. Display ties can be interpreted as tied competitive positions, changing points, credit awards or grid rotation. Local rules promise conversion to AI and allow humans-only fields, but the rules leave open whether converting the last active human continues the session unattended. A former human's camera, pause authority and finish obligation are also unresolved.

Adopt an event-start hard limit that accommodates recovery, plus the existing first-finisher deadlines inside it. Let all-terminal participants close the race early. Give the harness a separate bounded timeout that records incomplete entries and fails the deadlock check. Use exact crossing fractions for competitive order, define a single equal-time places/rewards rule, and distinguish times that merely round to the same displayed value. Specify DNF and exact-tie values used in cup tiebreaks. Make the slot lifecycle explicit across active, disconnected, reassigned, converted, finished and retired states. For local withdrawal keep the participant ID and car for AI conversion, transfer host authority explicitly, and define a terminal outcome when no humans remain. For solo career preserve the documented pending event on human withdrawal. State how humans-only fields behave after a withdrawal. Freeze a Local Cup availability and loadout matrix, including whether guests can enter all four schedules immediately, which Garage/loaner combinations are legal, and what remains fixed between rounds.

**Milestones and verification —** M3/M4/M6. Table-test no finish, same-tick finish, equal displayed times, reconnect near a deadline, host and last-human withdrawal, and cup continuation after DNF. Check standings, cameras, timers, credits and unlocks against the same result snapshot.

### 5. Reward eligibility and records are underspecified at persistence boundaries

**Severity — Major.** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:19–31,69–81,149–176` (Drift, Modes, Upgrades, Career), `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:74–86,114–122` (Content contracts, Persistence and records), `PLAN.md:143–154,160–165` (M6/M7).

The plan caps drift credits at 100 without defining what makes drifting varied or how score converts to credits. Arcade Rush specifies a total allowance, which can still leave an impossible opening checkpoint. Time Attack specifies metadata and separate garage records but leaves medal eligibility, exact record grouping and valid-lap invalidation on loadout/assist changes open. A strong-assist stock lap could silently replace a weaker-assist comparison in one implementation and have a separate best in another. Finally, a unique event ID and atomic replacement do not specify how a finish survives a crash before the first valid save write or which generation wins when the main file and backup disagree.

Specify qualifying drift as a consumer of validated grounded progress through signed turn zones. Define anti-repeat/cut checks, score-to-credit rounding, and whether contact cancels the whole drift or just its current attempt. Author each Arcade Rush deadline against cumulative reference splits, including the standing start, and define expiry at the exact tick of crossing. Freeze the record key and restrict incompatible assist/loadout/revision comparisons, with explicit stock medal eligibility and time/drift record rules for Arcade Rush. For career, assign event IDs before play and persist an immutable pending result or one complete new profile generation with the applied ID and all resulting state. Acknowledge a result only after the chosen durability boundary. Define startup selection/replay and the exact pre-commit crash behavior, then compact only IDs that can no longer be replayed. Preserve a single writer or explicitly arbitrate two game instances. Godot documents buffer flushing at file close. Verify replacement, backup and crash recovery on each target filesystem rather than inferring guarantees from that API. [Godot FileAccess](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_fileaccess.html#class-fileaccess-method-flush)

**Milestones and verification —** M1 fixes the drift event contract, M5 proves the save boundary, M6/M7 cover all modes. Repeat interruption, disk-write error and duplicate-result fixtures per platform. Test assist changes, previous records, arcade deadlines and shortest versus longest legal credits per minute. State the primary-save/backup outcome expected at each interruption point.

### 6. Split-screen is plausible and needs representative renderer, input and mix gates

**Severity — Major.** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:5–11,104–112,124–139,168–172` (Platform and rendering, Split-screen and audio, Budgets, Packaging), `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:184–200` (Presentation, Local multiplayer, Accessibility), `PLAN.md:55–63,91–101,184–196` (M0, M3, M9).

The proposed Godot 4.7.2 Standard release and matching templates exist. Sharing a World3D is supported and keeps one authoritative simulation. Each active camera still submits its own visible scene. At 1080p the four quadrants can sum to one screen's pixels while independent views multiply submissions, culling and some effect costs. M0's Mac renderer comparison can screen APIs, but an empty boot scene cannot lock the renderer for all OSes. Render time alone can also hide a refresh miss when VSync, shader work or a slow simulation changes presented cadence. Four audio listener weights and a voice cap leave unclear how equally important engines and checkpoint cues survive loud rival or waterfall layers in stereo and mono. An input device ID also needs a lifetime rule when two identical controllers reconnect.

Pin machine/driver, physics backend, renderer, internal scale, effects, VSync and measurement method. Lock the default after a representative M3 capture on the intended reference profiles. Count active viewports, presented frame intervals, CPU/GPU times and simulation tails. Set one default 3D scale openly and test all presets, including native HUD at 150%. Use a shared priority policy with per-player engine audibility, cue reservations/coalescing, a smoothed mix and explicit mono behavior. Treat connection IDs as transient and require a fresh deliberate reassignment where devices are ambiguous. Validate exported packages with actual controllers and keyboard in each advertised OS. Godot documents that downloaded macOS packages can encounter Gatekeeper and that notarization needs a Developer ID certificate. Test a fresh downloaded, quarantined package on macOS and distinguish the documented ad-hoc route from notarized offline launch. [Godot 4.7.2 archive](https://godotengine.org/download/archive/4.7.2-stable/), [Viewport](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_viewport.html), [Godot renderer overview](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/renderers.html), [Controller support](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/inputs/controllers_gamepads_joysticks.html), [macOS export](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/export/exporting_for_macos.html)

**Milestones and verification —** M0, M3 and M5/M8/M9. Run experiment 4. Exercise both four-player input configurations, three-player spare panel, mixed spectator/live views, regaining focus with a held trigger, and cue intelligibility at default and reduced dynamic range.

### 7. Content and balance gates need a costed second proof before bulk production

**Severity — Major.** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:29–35,105–120,128–153,168–176` (Handling, Courses, Cars, Career), `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md:32–89` (Art, Inventory, Graphics, Effects and Music), `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:141–166` (Verification matrix), `PLAN.md:129–141,156–188` (M5–M9).

M7 already requires validating a second course, but its completion criteria do not say how to decide whether recipe editing, regeneration and independent review can scale to the inventory. A second route that needs generator forks or repeated hand alignment by agents can pass geometry checks while exposing a production bottleneck. Font coverage alone also misses kerning, ambiguous glyphs and layout, especially for controller names and offline license text. Synth metering cannot distinguish six memorable themes from six variations of one phrase. The release harness nominally runs 1,800 races. At 3–5 simulated minutes each that represents roughly 90–150 simulation hours before extra AI tests or retries. Actual wall time depends on accelerated throughput. Length and lap time targets also need coupling. For example, 4.3 km in 60 seconds requires a 258 km/h average, beyond the proposed stock envelope, and even 95 seconds requires about 163 km/h average. A 60-second goal for the longest course is infeasible at the stated caps. The overall 60–95-second range leaves room to assign different targets by course.

Adopt course-specific pace baselines, including speed traces, braking sightlines, combined grade/bank/crest cases and the narrowest two-car envelope. Extend the same-car grip/drift comparison beyond one sweeper to tight sequences, mixed surfaces and upgraded presets. Keep the standard-finish economy accessible and measure whether short Quick Race has a material rewards-per-minute advantage from fixed overhead or route length. Put an early second-course/cars/music/type production proof at the front of M7 and halt bulk generation until review and regeneration effort can be estimated. Use synthetic telemetry for mechanics, full-speed comparative captures and audio listening for experience, and bounded player feedback where available. Reuse seeded runs between release gates only where the tested configuration and metrics match. Partition fast checks, milestone subsets and release batches using measured throughput.

**Milestones and verification —** M1/M2 for handling/sightlines, M5/M7 for production, M6/M8 for pacing and regression cost. Run experiments 2 and 5. Report route-specific pace and asset revision effort rather than extrapolating a single successful track or tune.

### 8. Rights policy needs an enforceable exception path and artifact lineage

**Severity — Major.** `LICENSE:1–24`, `README.md:15–19` (Authorship, License), `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md:5–30,69–75,91–127` (License scope, Authorship, External material, Manifest and release), `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md:33–57` (Training, Model and data rights), `PLAN.md:21–30,53–63,174–196` (Commitments, M0, M8/M9).

The Unlicense/CC0 split and preservation of exact engine notices are sound. The baseline permits only exceptional external CC0 creative material, while additional-language glyphs may be “properly licensed” and engine fallbacks may be packaged under actual terms. A required creative font could therefore enter by being called a dependency. Provider records also need to say whether regeneration from source is actually possible. Recording a prompt and committing an output establish different things. A generated score using an external soundfont, or a model that consumes an unreviewed trace, can have valid first-party hashes while containing unapproved inputs.

Make first-party recipes/assets the default as specified. Require an explicit scope decision for external creative substitutions, including criteria for identifying engine-bundled components and game-selected creative inputs. Trace source-to-output inputs for each glyph, sample, generator, encoder, model/checkpoint and dataset, including inventory or regenerated source instructions for synthetic data. Record which verified terms apply to each artifact and which authorized people or entities can dedicate applicable rights. Keep the report of authorship distinct from a legal grant. Define reproducibility classes for deterministic recipes, statistically reproducible training, and optional service output with frozen original files and verified redistribution rights. Compare actual exports and source bundles against independently reviewed manifests and the pinned runtime notice inventory. CC0 includes a fallback license and has limits on which rights it reaches. [Unlicense and contribution guidance](https://unlicense.org/), [CC0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode), [Godot compliance guide](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/about/complying_with_licenses.html)

**Milestones and verification —** M0 defines the policy and contributor record before intake, M5 checks the slice's shipped assets, M7/M8/M9 audit full packages. Test that unknown lineage, unapproved fallback/sample inputs and missing runtime notices fail release validation.

## Cross-document consistency

- **Simulation ownership** — The technical movement plan and AI's exact-step requirement need a shared tick API and early validation (findings 1–2).
- **Race and profile state** — Game modes and local slot behavior need one terminal result contract for RaceSession, cup points, and durable progression (findings 4–5).
- **AI action and fairness** — Classic tactical authoring and learned-policy acceptance depend on one action/observation schema and evaluation at the same realized-maneuver layer (finding 3).
- **Platform and content gates** — The M0 renderer decision needs to remain provisional until it has M3 workload evidence. Measured route pace and content throughput need to feed M7 authoring and M8 verification cost (findings 6–7).
- **Origin and release claims** — Engine/default glyph handling and exceptional creative inputs need one policy across the scoped dedication and package audit (finding 8).

## Optional improvements

- Add a compact comparison reel to M5/M7 evidence showing each route's approach, braking cue and reveal from chase and split-screen cameras. Use it alongside driving footage to catch repeated composition.
- Add an opt-in developer overlay to plot braking points, intended and executed AI maneuvers, and interventions against track progress. This can reuse existing telemetry.
- **Scope change, contingent only** — If the second production proof establishes a genuine release constraint and the owner prefers an earlier version, consider deferring some car/course bundles to a later release. That would require explicitly revising the cup schedule, unlock graph, asset counts and AI generalization gate. This review recommends retaining all six courses, twelve cars, five modes, native targets, four-player local play and genuine learned rivals in the current plan.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- Use a shared fixed-tick race and one player/AI command path. It gives fairness and input/AI diagnostics a common point of verification.
- Bake road geometry and gameplay metadata from authored recipes. Deliberate route rhythm and scenic composition can coexist with mechanical road validation and reproducible regeneration.
- Keep stock loaners, separate solo progression and sampled ghosts. These choices make a new local group immediately playable and reduce the impact of cross-platform determinism limits.
- Learn tactics with a small local actor and shared follower and safety layer. Retain the matched Classic baseline, holdouts, intervention reporting, and exceptional fallback.
- Require full-build visual, audio and play review alongside automated tests. Preserve offline packaging, original editable source material and explicit third-party notices.
