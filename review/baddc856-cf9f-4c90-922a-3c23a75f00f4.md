### Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.** Confidence: moderately high.

The racing core (arcade motor, checkpoint-authoritative progress, race state, coordinated car contacts, economy, split-screen ownership, persistence, licensing) is specified completely and consistently enough that an implementation agent would not need to invent essential rules. Every external claim I could check is accurate.

The revisions concentrate in two places. First, the learned Roadstormers have no reproducible measurement protocol and no bounded budget, although they are required launch scope. Second, the plan's most important experiential gates use benchmark terms that have no operational definition. None of the fixes requires a redesign. The single option that would change requested scope is labelled as such in finding 2.

### Ranked findings

#### 1. Roadstormer acceptance gates are not reproducibly measurable as written

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 13, 20–25, 39, 65–79; `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 91–99; `PLAN.md` lines 119–127.
- **Problem:**
  - The Behavioral distinction gate (line 71) needs "at least 200 matched opportunity windows per pair". A window is never defined, and nothing makes windows matched. The policy observes "a short recent history of pass attempts and contacts" (line 25), so the behaviour the feature exists for (line 13's lane-closes-twice example) cannot occur in a window that starts from a reset. Godot's physics is not deterministic, so replaying a scripted lead-in does not guarantee the same hand-over state either. The bridge contract exposes reset and step only (line 39); there is no mid-episode state snapshot.
  - Difficulty is undefined for learned rivals. `GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 91–97 define it as a percentage of a reference pace for Classic planning. For Roadstormers the document says only that the policy proposes a "target pace within the safe envelope" (line 20). If difficulty is applied as a multiplier after the policy, it alters proposed pace by up to about 15%, which line 79 counts as a "material correction" above 5%, contaminating the Safety interventions gate (line 74).
  - "Heavy-contact rate" (line 69) has no threshold. Line 79 predefines only lane and pace corrections.
- **Failure example:** Two agents each report a passing M4B on evidence that cannot be compared. A policy passes the safety gate only because difficulty scaling was left out of the count, or fails only because it was included.
- **Correction:** Add an evaluation-protocol contract to M4B.1 and freeze it before any PPO run:
  1. Window format: a deterministic scripted lead-in, a hand-over tick, a definition of what triggers an opportunity, a window length, and a seeded history buffer.
  2. Matching rule: either a snapshot and restore capability in the bridge, or a stated hand-over tolerance (for example at most 0.25 m and 0.5 m/s, with the window discarded beyond it).
  3. Difficulty mechanism. Recommended: difficulty as a normalized policy input that is randomized during training, reaction delay applied by the safety layer, and interventions measured against the difficulty-scaled proposal.
  4. One contact-severity scale, defined with the resolver in M1.4, with the heavy threshold fixed before evaluation.
- **Milestones and verification:** M4B.1 sign-off includes the protocol. M4B.6 implements it. M4B.7 and M8.7 report against it. T10 references it. Experiment 2 resolves the matching rule.

#### 2. The Roadstormer programme has no budget, hardware profile, or stop rule, yet is required launch scope

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 5, 45, 81; `PLAN.md` lines 49, 117, 120–121, 212, 223.
- **Problem:** The plan bounds only the throughput benchmark (10,000–100,000 decision steps) and defers the pilot cap to that measurement. Nothing states the training machine, a cap per run, the number of runs, or what happens when the gate fails. Line 5 says to "continue the training/design milestone".
- **Estimate (mine, to be replaced by the M4B.2 measurement):** A three-lap race at about 80 seconds per lap is roughly 1,200 decision steps at 5 Hz. A PPO run of a few million decision steps is therefore tens of millions of physics ticks. The plan implies well over ten runs: demonstration generation, at least three seeds for each of two styles, and the M7.7 retrain. The only named machine is an 8 GB Apple M1 (`docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:126`), which limits how many Godot workers fit beside PyTorch.
- **Failure example:** An agent with no authority to buy compute spends an unbounded period inside M4B, or weakens the gate to finish. M5 depends on M4B, so the vertical slice is blocked either way.
- **Correction:** After M4B.2, record a budget table: training machine, per-run caps in decision steps and wall time, worker count, the full run matrix for M4B and M7.7, and total wall time. Write the stop rule as an owner decision: if the gate fails within budget, the owner chooses between one more budgeted revision round and a scope change, such as shipping Roadstormers in a post-release update. **That second option changes requested scope** and must be recorded as a scope change (`PLAN.md:229`).
- **Milestones and verification:** M4B.2 produces the budget table. M4B.7 and M7.7 record actual cost against it. Experiment 1 should run right after M1 so the budget is known well before M4B.

#### 3. Handling is finalised after the learned policies are trained

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** `PLAN.md` lines 117, 134, 139, 166, 174, 180; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 51; `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 122.
- **Problem:** Line 51 of `OPPONENT_AI.md` says a handling revision "invalidates affected training/evaluation results and requires retraining or demonstrated compatibility". M4B is declared dependent on "stable handling/content revisions" (`PLAN.md:117`). Yet M5.2 then finishes "the three starter cars with distinct handling" (line 134), and M8.1 tunes "car parity, drift versus grip, upgrades" (line 174) after the final training in M7.7 (line 166). M5.7 and M8.7 therefore evaluate policies against handling that may no longer match their training, and neither M5 nor M8 contains a retraining task or budget.
- **Failure example:** Full-content validation reveals a parity problem in M8.1. Fixing it invalidates the shipped checkpoint's evaluation at the end of the schedule, with no budgeted way to re-evaluate or retrain.
- **Correction:** Add an explicit handling-freeze gate. Freeze starter-car handling resources before M4B.3; any later change re-opens M4B parity and evaluation. Freeze all car handling before M7.7. Restrict M8.1 to non-physical parameters (AI pace fractions, rewards, cup difficulty, Arcade Rush timings) unless a recorded decision reopens physics, in which case M8.7 carries an explicit re-evaluation and retraining budget.
- **Milestones and verification:** Model cards and ghost metadata already record handling revisions; add a release check (extend T15) asserting they equal the shipped revision.

#### 4. The drift reward is numerically invisible, and the remaining drift-reward rules are undefined

- **Severity:** major
- **Where:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 29, 31, 51, 79, 97, 174; `PLAN.md` line 220.
- **Problem:** The surge is "up to 4% added acceleration for 0.5 seconds" (line 29). The speed gain is 0.04 × acceleration × 0.5 s. With arcade acceleration at corner-exit speed of roughly 3–8 m/s² (my assumption; the plan gives no curve), that is 0.06–0.16 m/s, about 0.2–0.6 km/h, or 0.1–0.4% of a 40 m/s exit speed. At 40 m/s that is worth roughly 10–30 ms over the following 300 m, less once drag erodes it. The parity gate tolerates ±5%, which is about ±0.5 s on a ten-second corner. The stated reward is one to two orders of magnitude smaller than the tolerance it sits inside, and far below what a player can feel.
- **Related gaps:**
  - Drafting gives "up to 2% drag reduction" (line 51), about +1 km/h after a three-second tow at 180 km/h and only below the speed cap. Line 97 still lists drafting as a personality dimension.
  - "Style rewards are capped" (line 31) and "up to 100 for varied controlled drifting" (line 174) have no formula: no point rate, no cap, no definition of "varied". Credits depend on this, and Arcade Rush stores a drift score (line 79).
- **Failure example:** An agent implements the numbers literally. The parity gate passes. Drifting has no tangible payoff, which is the first risk in `PLAN.md`'s table (line 220).
- **Correction:** State the surge in a unit with a perceptibility target (for example +3 to +5 km/h, or an acceleration multiplier of 1.5 to 2 for 0.5–1 s) and re-run the parity gate at that magnitude. Define the style score (points per second inside the slip band, per drift or per combo, and the cap) and the credit formula (for example, "varied" means qualifying drifts on at least N distinct corners). Either give drafting a measurable effect or drop it as a personality axis.
- **Status:** The arithmetic is confirmed for the stated units. The perceptibility threshold needs experiment 3. Drift never being faster than grip is a documented design choice, not a defect; the problem is that it currently has no reward at all.
- **Milestones and verification:** M1.3 and M1 acceptance; M6.6 (economy); M8.1.

- **Where:** `PLAN.md` lines 76, 113, 182; `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 29, 79, 93–99; `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 149.
- **Problem:**

### Feasibility experiments

These are proposals. None was run during this review.

1. **Headless stepping throughput probe**
   - Inputs: the M1 laboratory scene and graybox Riviera, eight cars with scripted or Classic drivers, Godot 4.7.2 headless, the designated training machine.
   - Cost limit: at most 1 agent-day and 4 machine-hours; no training.
   - Pass: at least 50 decision steps per second per worker (600 physics ticks per second) with four or more concurrent workers inside 8 GB, or a measured number from which the finding 2 budget totals at most 40 machine-hours for the whole run matrix.
   - Decision: the training architecture (workers per process, batching several scenarios per process, whether GDScript stepping is viable) and the finding 2 budget. Run it before any bridge work.
2. **Bridge repeatability and state-fork test**
   - Inputs: the M4B.2 bridge prototype and one scripted scenario.
   - Cost limit: at most 2 agent-days.
   - Pass: either a reset followed by 600 identical-command steps twice diverges by at most 0.1 m and 0.2 m/s at hand-over in at least 95 of 100 trials, or a full-state snapshot and restore reproduces the next 120 ticks in 100 of 100 trials.
   - Decision: whether matched windows use scripted lead-ins with a tolerance or require the fork capability (finding 1).
3. **Drift reward perceptibility**
   - Inputs: the M1 laboratory, the benchmark sweeper, and four surge variants (the specified 4% for 0.5 s, +3 km/h, +5 km/h, and doubled acceleration for 1 s).
   - Cost limit: inside M1 tuning, at most 1 agent-day.
   - Pass: parity within 5% at the chosen magnitude, and a reviewer comparing blind A/B captures identifies the rewarded run in at least 8 of 10 pairs. The specified 4% variant is expected to fail.
   - Decision: the surge specification at `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:29`.
4. **Learned-style separation pilot**
   - Inputs: behavioural cloning on Classic demonstrations, then one-seed PPO on Riviera segments, for each style.
   - Cost limit: at most 1,000,000 decision steps or 8 wall hours per style, whichever comes first.
   - Pass: under the finding 1 protocol on at least 50 windows per pair, Surge against Switch shows at least 25% meaningful differences, material corrections stay below 5%, and pace is within 10% of Classic.
   - Decision: whether the observation and reward design is viable before the three-seed production runs; feeds the finding 2 budget.
5. **Four-camera renderer comparison**
   - Inputs: the M3.4 stress scene, Compatibility and Mobile, one and four views, rear view held, both reference profiles.
   - Cost limit: at most 2 agent-days.
   - Pass: p95 at most 16.7 ms and p99 at most 25 ms at default 1080p settings in the worst case (`docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:130–131`).
   - Decision: the renderer lock and the rear-view implementation (finding 7).

### Optional improvements

These are not required for the plan to be implementable.

- Run experiment 1 immediately after M1, two milestones before M4B, so a GDScript throughput problem is known while an architecture change is still cheap.
- Add recognition gates for the two most subjective generated assets: an independent reviewer matches each car silhouette at split-screen size to its character sheet, and HUD strings pass a legibility check at 20 and 24 pixels. Prototyping the glyph set in M2 instead of M5.5 would make a weak font cheaper to replace.
- State that `RaceWorld` advances every car in a fixed participant order from a single physics callback (`docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md:70`). The shared tick-start snapshot and the bridge's exact tick count are easier to test once that is written down.
- Request owner resources once in M0 instead of discovering them late: four gamepads (M3), Windows 11 and Ubuntu 24.04 machines or virtual machines (M8, M9), and an Apple Developer ID if notarization is wanted (M9.3).
- Configure per-viewport shadow atlases deliberately; Godot's default on desktop is 4096 per viewport, which multiplies across four views.
- **Scope observations.** I recommend no change to the engine choice, the six circuits, the twelve cars, the five modes, or the three platforms. Godot with GDScript fits the requirements (MIT engine, shared-world viewports, headless scripting); its one project-specific risk is stepping throughput for training, covered by experiment 1. The content total is large for agent production, but the second-course gate in M2 and the vertical slice in M5 contain the risk. The only recommendation that changes requested scope is the Roadstormer contingency in finding 2.

### Strong decisions to preserve

- **Hierarchical opponents.** Learning handles tactics only; a shared low-level controller and a safety layer execute them with the same physics as the player, and there is no catch-up. This keeps the learned feature small, fair, and testable.
- **Ordered swept gates as the authority for progress**, with physical distance kept distinct from validated progress. It rules out overpass and shortcut exploits by construction.
- **A custom arcade motor on `CharacterBody3D`** rather than `VehicleBody3D`. Godot's own documentation supports this choice.
- **One coordinated car-contact resolver** fed by a shared tick-start snapshot, with engine car-to-car response excluded. It avoids double responses and is the right place for the contact rules.
- **Simulation separated from presentation**, with one-way events. Scenery state is owned by the simulation, so training, ghosts, and four cameras cannot diverge.
- **Candour about evidence.** The plan repeatedly distinguishes automated checks from experience review, declines to promise determinism, keeps hardware gates open when hardware is missing, and treats AI authorship as a provenance record rather than a clearance.
- **Vertical slice before content**, with the second course validated before the remaining four.
