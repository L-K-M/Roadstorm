Relevant external spot checks:

- Godot's official archive does list **4.7.2-stable** (18 August 2026), so the proposed pin exists.
- Godot's license page confirms that game content may use a separate license while the distributed engine binary and components require their own notices; the plan's caution is accurate.
- Godot's viewport documentation confirms that viewports can share a `World3D` and that each viewport has independently configurable 2D/3D audio-listener processing. This supports the shared-world rendering premise but leaves the split-screen audio mixing decision to the project.
- Stable Baselines3's PPO documentation lists `Discrete`, `Box`, `MultiDiscrete`, and `MultiBinary` action spaces as supported, but not `Dict` as an action space. A mixed continuous/categorical tactical action therefore needs an explicit compatible encoding.
- CC0 is applied by an “Affirmer” only to rights that affirmer owns or controls. The Unlicense's contribution guidance likewise recommends explicit dedications from major contributors. U.S. Copyright Office guidance treats copyright as requiring human authorship and evaluates AI-assisted works case by case; wholly machine-generated material is not registrable in the U.S. This does not resolve rights or dedications in other jurisdictions.

## Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.**

Confidence: moderate-high. The plan is unusually coherent in scope control, milestone ordering, simulation ownership, fairness constraints, provenance, and risk gating. I found no evidence that Godot, small offline learned policies, or four-player split-screen are categorically infeasible for this design. However, an unfamiliar implementation agent would still have to invent several essential shared rules: measurable handling/upgrade semantics, AI reference pace, the learned policy's action/reward/difficulty contract, parts of the economy, and the split-screen audio ownership model. Some acceptance gates are not yet objectively scoreable, and the release verification batch has no execution-time budget. These are specific correctable deficiencies rather than proof that the project cannot work.

## Ranked findings

### 1. The vehicle handling and upgrade contract is behavioral but not mechanically measurable

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, “Driving experience” lines 17–31, “Cars and garage” lines 130–145, “Upgrades” lines 151–155; `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`, “Movement and contacts” lines 62–72.
- **Problem:** the documents describe desired feel and list state variables, but never define the versioned handling-resource fields or how the 1–5 car scores map to them. Upgrade effects use undefined quantities such as “lateral response/recovery rate” and “steering response.” The benchmark sweeper geometry, entry conditions, and fixed-tick comparison tolerance are also absent, even though M1 acceptance depends on them (`PLAN.md` lines 69–76).
- **Failure example:** one implementer treats Tires as higher lateral damping, making a drift car harder to slide; another treats it as faster yaw recovery only. Both can claim a “+5%” implementation while producing different handling identities. Likewise, two agents can choose different sweepers and both claim grip and drift are within 5%.
- **Proposed correction:** do not freeze final tuning now, but do freeze the schema and measurement contract before M1 implementation: acceleration curve/top speed, yaw response versus speed, lateral damping in grip and drift, drift entry/exit thresholds and blend rates, surface coefficients, mass/contact dimensions, airborne behavior, upgrade stacking equations, and the exact benchmark corner/car/assist/entry-speed setup. Map each 1–5 design score to an allowed parameter range and define which telemetry demonstrates preserved identity.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M1, M6, M8; T02, T03, T12. Verify with boundary tests, telemetry traces, the specified sweeper, stock/maximum-upgrade matrices, and side-by-side captured driving.

### 2. AI difficulty percentages have no defined reference driver, and learned-rival difficulty control is unspecified

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, “Fair and assertive opponents” lines 91–99; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md`, architecture lines 20–27 and acceptance lines 68–73.
- **Problem:** Rookie/Racer/Expert are percentages of a “validated safe reference pace” produced by a “documented reference driver,” but no such driver, measurement protocol, car/course configuration, or statistical treatment is defined anywhere. Expert's 100–103% target is circular if the reference is already near the physical optimum. Separately, a Roadstormer policy outputs target pace, yet the documents do not say whether difficulty is a policy input, an external pace cap, separate checkpoints, or a reaction-delay mechanism.
- **Failure example:** a conservative reference hot-lap bot makes “Expert” slower than a competent player; an ideal-line bot makes even Rookie's speed selection inconsistent. A Rookie Roadstormer may also choose the fastest safe pace its policy learned, silently defeating the intended difficulty.
- **Proposed correction:** define the reference driver before M4 tuning: exact track revision, stock reference car/configuration, assists off, no traffic, deterministic controller or documented human/bot procedure, number of laps, and whether pace is a clean-lap mean, median, or percentile. State how each difficulty scales that reference and how reaction delay is applied. For Roadstormers, choose either an explicit difficulty input plus training randomization or a hard external pace/reaction envelope, then test all three difficulties.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4, M4B, M8; T10 and T11. Verification needs reference-lap publication, per-difficulty pace distributions, reaction-delay tests, and mixed-field review.

### 3. The Roadstormer observation/action/reward contract is too underdetermined for consistent implementation

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md`, “Architecture” lines 19–31 and “Training approach” lines 35–51; `PLAN.md`, M4B lines 119–127.
- **Problem:** the exact feature list is deferred, while `TacticalIntent` combines lane/offset and pace (naturally continuous) with follow/pass commitment and drift preference (potentially categorical). Stable Baselines3 PPO does not accept a general `Dict` action space, so a naive implementation of that mixed intent is not directly compatible. The plan also lacks the action encoding, clipping/rate limits, reward weights, termination/truncation rules, demonstration volume/quality criteria, and difficulty/personality input representation. Flattening a short history can also conflict with the sub-10,000-parameter target if the already broad observation vector is replicated for several steps.
- **Failure example:** one agent implements a hybrid dictionary action and discovers that standard SB3 PPO cannot train it; another quantizes every output into `MultiDiscrete`, but behavioral-cloning labels and runtime scaling no longer match. Reward weights chosen ad hoc could produce technically valid but boring imitation or unsafe passing behavior.
- **Proposed correction:** make M4B.1 a written contract with minimum required content rather than an open-ended freeze: exact observation vector and history encoding; parameter count calculation; a PPO-compatible action space; decoding into `TacticalIntent`; rate and commitment limits; personality/difficulty inputs; safety-layer boundary; reward component weights and scale; episode termination versus truncation; demonstration scenario/count requirements; and export preprocessing/postprocessing. Review that contract before bridge or trainer implementation.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4B, M5, M7, M8; T10. Verification should include shape/property tests, deliberate unsafe proposals, BC/PPO serialization parity, and the export parity corpus.

### 4. The learned-opponent acceptance metrics are not yet objectively scoreable

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md`, “Implementation and acceptance” lines 63–81.
- **Problem:** “Heavy-contact rate” has no contact-severity threshold. “No worse ... by more than 10%” does not say relative percentage or percentage points; the promised small absolute allowance is undefined. “Meaningful differences” explicitly excludes raw numeric differences but supplies no labeling rubric, opportunity-window detector, denominator, or reviewer agreement procedure. The denominator for material corrections is also ambiguous between 5 Hz tactical proposals and 60 Hz low-level corrections.
- **Failure example:** an evaluator can pass a weak policy by counting only long passing opportunities, while another counts every frame alongside and fails it. A heavily guard-repaired policy can appear behaviorally distinct even though the raw network contributes little.
- **Proposed correction:** pre-register the evaluation protocol before training results are inspected: scenario families and seeds, exact opportunity-start/end events, contact impulse/velocity thresholds, paired Classic baseline selection, relative versus absolute statistical criteria, confidence intervals, intervention denominators, and a categorical decision rubric for pass/follow, side, commit/wait, and drift entry. Report both raw-policy and post-safety behavior.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4B, M7, M8; T10. Verification consists of the pre-registered evaluation, deliberate unsafe-input tests, matched Classic races, and independent clip review.

### 5. Race verification and evaluation workload lacks an execution-time budget or acceleration contract

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`, verification matrix lines 145–162, especially T11 line 157; `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, standard-race duration line 57; `PLAN.md`, M4 acceptance line 113.
- **Problem:** T11 alone specifies 100 seeds × six tracks × three difficulties, or 1,800 races. At the planned three-to-five-minute race duration, that is 90–150 hours of simulated race time per sequential real-time runner, before adding M4's 300 slice races, car/course compatibility runs, endurance tests, and learned-AI evaluation. The documents never state whether the harness may run faster than real time, how many parallel workers are expected, what accuracy constraints apply, or what wall-clock/core-hour budget is acceptable.
- **Failure example:** the full matrix becomes expensive enough that a team substitutes a small informal sample, or release validation is delayed until after content is already locked. Intermittent deadlocks then escape precisely because the required breadth was impractical.
- **Proposed correction:** specify an accelerated headless race harness early: maximum wall time and core-hours, worker count, real-time factor, fixed-tick accuracy requirements, result reconciliation, and which tests must remain real-time. Use stratified smoke tests during development and a statistically justified full matrix at release rather than treating race count itself as the quality measure.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4, M4B, M7, M8; T10–T14. Add a throughput benchmark in M0/M4 and record achieved simulation seconds per CPU second with each full gate.

### 6. Drift rewards and race-length scaling do not define the economy they are meant to calibrate

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, drift rules lines 29–31, race length line 57, rewards line 174; `PLAN.md`, M6 acceptance line 154.
- **Problem:** “up to 100 for varied controlled drifting” has no formula for awarding or diminishing style credit, and “varied” is not operationalized beyond prohibitions on obvious farming. Quick Race rewards scale by laps, but Career races may have per-course lap counts and no corresponding scaling rule is given. Therefore the acceptance claims that one standard finish buys the first upgrade and about eight typical finishes fully upgrade a favorite cannot be tested as written.
- **Failure example:** long repetitive drifts in a few favorable corners always reach the 100-credit cap, halving the intended upgrade time; alternatively, conservative implementation makes drift credit negligible. A one-lap Career event could pay a three-lap reward and dominate credits per minute.
- **Proposed correction:** define the drift-credit equation, including qualifying-turn identity, angle/time/progress contribution, diminishing returns, cooldowns, wall/off-road invalidation, and examples. Apply a single explicit lap/duration scaling rule to every credit-paying mode, with rounding examples and transaction test vectors.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M6 and M8; T02 and T06. Verify economy simulations, exploit cases, shortest-event credits per minute, and exact ledger outcomes.

### 7. Four-player audio ownership is named but not architected

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`, “Split-screen and audio” lines 104–112; `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, audio lines 180–185; `PLAN.md`, M3.4 line 98 and M5.4 line 136.
- **Problem:** the plan requires “an original shared sound-priority system” but does not decide whether each `SubViewport` is an audio listener, whether positional emitters produce one logical voice or per-player representations, how panning/attenuation are combined, or how voices are allocated and stolen. The 32-voice budget is not allocated by category. Naively crossfading idle/load/coast loops for eight cars can consume most or all of that budget before contacts, scenery, UI, and crowd layers.
- **Failure example:** player four cannot hear their own engine because nearer rival engines consume the cap; alternatively, the same collision is emitted once per listener and creates a loud phasey accumulation. A waterfall audible only to one player either becomes global or disappears entirely.
- **Proposed correction:** write an audio-event contract before M3: listener model, logical emitter ownership, per-player weights, category budgets, priority and stealing rules, engine-loop voice accounting, ducking, positional-versus-global cue policy, and pause behavior. Define whether music/UI are outside the 32-voice 3D budget and provide a measurable four-player mix test.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M3, M5, M8; T13 and T14. Verify with synthetic voice-count tests, recorded four-player mixes, loudness/headroom measurements, and subjective review.

### 8. The public-domain rights chain is acknowledged but not actually closed

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md`, “License scope” lines 7–22, “Authorship and contribution records” lines 26–30, and release gate lines 121–127; `PLAN.md`, M0.3 line 57.
- **Problem:** the plan correctly says that an agent cannot create legal authority, but it never names the human or legal entity who will act as affirmer/contributor for rights that do arise in code, art, music, datasets, or model weights. A generated provenance statement is not itself a dedication. U.S. guidance makes wholly AI-generated material uncopyrightable, but human selection/arrangement, jurisdiction-specific rights, database rights, and service terms can still matter. CC0 and the Unlicense only dedicate rights controlled by the person or entity making the dedication.
- **Failure example:** thousands of assets carry agent-generated “dedicated to CC0” records while no person or owner entity with authority ever executed a blanket dedication. The release claim becomes difficult to substantiate even if the artifacts are in fact original.
- **Proposed correction:** in M0, have the project owner or appropriate legal entity execute an explicit blanket Unlicense/CC0 dedication for rights they control in contributions produced under their direction, and reference that instrument in contribution records. Separately record provider output terms, human selection/arrangement where relevant, and uncertain/no-rights status rather than overstating clearance. If the public-domain claim is a hard requirement, obtain qualified legal review before release.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M0, M5, M8, M9; T15. Verification is the rights audit plus inspection of the executed dedication and dependency/service-term inventory.

### 9. Displayed finish ties conflict with hidden finish ordering

- **Severity:** minor.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md`, “Standard race” line 61.
- **Problem:** ranking uses exact finish-crossing time and participant ID only for an exact internal tie, but also says to show a tie when displayed times coincide. If two cars finish 0.004 seconds apart and both display the same centisecond time, the rule does not say whether they share position/points or are secretly ordered by the unrounded time.
- **Failure example:** two players see identical finish times but receive different positions and cup points without an explanation; or they are shown as tied while the underlying points silently use participant ID.
- **Proposed correction:** choose one official rule: either quantize finish times for both ranking and display, or rank by exact time and never present rounded equality as a tie. Define shared-position points if true ties remain possible.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4 and M6; T05/T06. Test simultaneous and near-simultaneous finishes at display precision boundaries.

### 10. Trademark/name review is deferred until after content production

- **Severity:** minor.
- **File and section:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md`, license caution line 22; `PLAN.md`, M9.6 line 193.
- **Problem:** the documents correctly state that public-domain dedication is not trademark clearance, but the only explicit title decision is in M9 after logo, car cards, audio/text references, packages, and promotional material exist. There is no scheduled screening for car names either.
- **Failure example:** a late conflict involving the product title or a car name forces asset regeneration, UI changes, and re-review immediately before release.
- **Proposed correction:** add a bounded name-screening decision before the M5 vertical slice and re-check before M7 content lock. Record that screening is not legal clearance and escalate for legal advice if a conflict matters.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M5, M7, M9. Verification is a decision record plus updated asset/manifest review for any renamed content.

## Feasibility experiments

1. **Handling-schema prototype**
   - **Inputs:** one specified graybox sweeper, hairpin, crest, three starter-car parameter sets, keyboard and one gamepad.
   - **Cost limit:** two agent-days; no content production or external dependencies.
   - **Pass/fail:** telemetry demonstrates the required drift states; grip and drift benchmark times are measurable under the written protocol; stock cars remain within the intended parity band; upgrades alter the defined parameters without erasing car identity; no nonfinite/tunneling failures in the specified abuse cases.
   - **Decision:** whether the proposed handling schema can represent the design before M1 is closed.

2. **Four-viewport rendering and audio spike**
   - **Inputs:** one graybox course section with representative generated scenery, eight moving cars, four cameras, synthesized engine/contact/environment loops, and the recorded reference hardware.
   - **Cost limit:** three agent-days plus one benchmark day per available machine.
   - **Pass/fail:** meets or truthfully misses the p95/p99 frame-time gates; worst-case voice counts fit the written allocation; recorded four-player audio preserves each local engine and priority cues without clipping or duplicated event spikes.
   - **Decision:** choose Compatibility versus Mobile rendering and the audio listener/event architecture before bulk content.

3. **Headless simulation acceleration and reset fidelity**
   - **Inputs:** one simple and one Riviera-like route, eight AI cars, a batch harness, and the proposed Godot training bridge.
   - **Cost limit:** one workday of implementation measurement and no more than eight core-hours of execution.
   - **Pass/fail:** one tactical step advances exactly twelve physics ticks; reset restores a predefined state hash; accelerated race outcomes agree with a real-time reference within declared tolerance; measure sustained simulation seconds per CPU second and memory growth.
   - **Decision:** set the T11 worker/wall-time budget and determine whether PPO training throughput is plausible before writing the full trainer.

4. **Bounded BC/PPO pilot**
   - **Inputs:** the frozen observation/action/reward contract, Classic-driver demonstrations on synthetic segments, one trainable slot, frozen Classic opponents, and a fixed local compute envelope.
   - **Cost limit:** at most 500,000 PPO decision steps or 24 core-hours after the throughput measurement, whichever comes first; no paid compute.
   - **Pass/fail:** BC transfer shapes are exact; PPO improves validation clean-pass/completion metrics over the BC initialization without raising safety interventions beyond the preregistered threshold; exported GDScript inference matches on the held-out corpus.
   - **Decision:** continue the current PPO design, simplify the action/reward contract, or stop before investing in full curriculum training.

5. **Vertical-slice media production pilot**
   - **Inputs:** one car, three representative props, one 60-second race loop, and one engine family produced entirely through the planned LLM-authored recipe pipeline.
   - **Cost limit:** two agent-days plus one independent review pass; no external creative assets.
   - **Pass/fail:** the car remains recognizable at split-screen size, prop silhouettes/palettes are distinguishable, manifests and regeneration hashes pass, audio meets the stated technical gates, and an independent reviewer rates the music/art as acceptable for the intended style rather than merely technically valid.
   - **Decision:** whether the procedural art/music direction is strong enough for six-course production or needs revision before M5/M7.

## Cross-document consistency

The major content counts are consistent across the documents: six courses, twelve cars, eight-car standard fields, two default Roadstormers, three starter cars, and the stated upgrade prices arithmetic all agree. The licensing split between Unlicense, CC0, and engine/dependency notices is also consistent in intent.

The substantive cross-document gaps are the missing shared contracts already identified above:

- handling parameters and upgrade vocabulary span `GAME_DESIGN.md` and `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` without a common measurable schema (finding 1);
- AI difficulty/reference pace spans `GAME_DESIGN.md`, `OPPONENT_AI.md`, and the verification matrix without a common reference measurement (finding 2);
- `TacticalIntent`, difficulty inputs, reward semantics, and evaluation categories are shared concerns across the opponent specification and milestones but are not frozen enough to implement independently (findings 3–4);
- “heavy contact” is needed by AI evaluation but not tied to the contact severity language in the game design (finding 4);
- audio ownership is a cross-cutting runtime/presentation contract currently represented only by a budget and high-level goals (finding 7);
- race-time precision must be shared by ranking, displayed results, and cup points (finding 9).

I did not find a direct contradiction in the Unlicense text itself; the root license matches the standard public-domain dedication wording.

## Optional improvements

These are not necessary corrections:

- **Scope-changing recommendation:** if M5 or M7 shows that quality is suffering, reduce launch content—for example fewer courses/cars or one learned style—rather than filling the game with weak generated content. This changes the requested six-course/twelve-car/two-style scope and should only be a deliberate owner decision.
- **Scope/policy-changing recommendation:** if the original geometric font or synthesized instruments remain weak after the media pilot, permit a narrowly verified CC0 font or sound source as an explicit external exception. This trades away part of the “all creative output newly LLM-produced” ideal for accessibility and audio quality.
- Add a concise decision-record template for handling, renderer, RL contract, and audio architecture so later agents do not rediscover why constraints were chosen.
- Define a compact diagnostic replay format rather than relying only on telemetry text and video captures.
- Use the same reference-lap tooling for AI difficulty, Time Attack medals, and Arcade Rush timing where practical, reducing inconsistent independent benchmarks.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- **Early risk gates before bulk content:** M1 handling, M3 four-player performance, M4B learned rivals, and M5 vertical slice are the right order. They prevent six-course production from hiding a weak core game.
- **Shared simulation separated from presentation:** one authoritative race world with view-specific rendering/audio consumers is essential for fair split-screen and testable results.
- **Ordered swept gates and route-local progress:** separating physical proximity from validated progress avoids the classic hairpin/overpass/shortcut failure modes of nearest-point racing logic.
- **No hidden rubber-band advantage:** requiring identical physical limits and prohibiting teleporting catch-up preserves trust and makes close racing legible.
- **Hierarchical learned opponents:** using a small policy for tactics while a shared controller handles driving is much more feasible than end-to-end driving and fits the desired behavioral distinction.
- **Offline fixed learned weights:** avoiding a runtime cloud/model service matches offline play, reproducibility, latency, cost, and release-rights requirements.
- **Explicit scope exclusions:** deferring online multiplayer, damage, complex tuning, dynamic weather physics, and similar features protects the requested core experience.
- **Provenance and rights gates:** treating source recipes, hashes, notices, and external exceptions as release requirements is a strong fit for a public-domain-oriented generated game.
- **Separation of metrics from experience review:** the repeated statement that bot completion, meters, and screenshots cannot prove fun or musical quality is important and should remain throughout production.
