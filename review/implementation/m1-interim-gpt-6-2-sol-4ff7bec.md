## Fixes required now

1. **Major — Tilted-wall response leaves inward road-plane velocity.** The adapter removes the inward component along the full 3D normal, then discards the vertical component that made the correction work. With zero restitution, tangential keep `1`, a wall normal `(√0.84, 0.4, 0)`, and an approach velocity `(-10, 0, 0)` m/s, the published velocity is `(-1.6, 0, 0)` m/s. Its dot product with the wall normal is still **−1.466 m/s**. The normal qualifies as wall-like under the `0.5` cutoff. Project the normal into the road plane and normalize it before resolving the response. Add a tilted-wall regression that checks the final committed velocity. `src/simulation/motor_body_adapter.gd:395–401, 503–512, 546–548, 660–676`.

2. **Major — The adapter considers only the first contact in each multi-contact result.** It requests up to eight contacts and reads only the default-index normal. A single substep whose collision result lists a floor or ramp contact first and a blocking wall second can stop the hull while publishing `wall_contact = false` and retaining inward velocity. Reproduce at a floor/wall junction with an inward approach, or inject one ordered contact batch at the collection seam. Iterate the reported contacts when collecting wall normals and preserve generic contact telemetry separately. The existing wall-then-floor control starts at the selector, downstream of the code that can drop the wall. This is a source-level reproduction path. The supplied Jolt evidence covers one wall contact. `src/simulation/motor_body_adapter.gd:385–401, 419–421, 503–512`; `tools/body_lab/body_lab_driver.gd:893–925, 991–1015`.

3. **Medium — A yaw-only obstruction can leave velocity pointing into the wall.** The motor constructs velocity in the fully proposed nose frame. The adapter can shorten the yaw, commit that shortened heading, and retain the fully rotated velocity when the translation had no wall hit. Start parallel to a wall, with no initial lateral velocity, and leave enough clearance for the translation chord but too little for the yawed corner. On the first constrained tick the car can acquire inward velocity even though the hull rotation that produced it was blocked. Define the velocity commit policy for this case and assert it alongside the final pose. The existing near-parallel regression checks hull clearance and heading agreement. `src/simulation/arcade_motor.gd:340–348`; `src/simulation/motor_body_adapter.gd:438–512`; `tools/body_lab/body_lab_driver.gd:1031–1108`.

4. **Medium — DriveLab can retain a held input after focus loss.** Its keyboard path stores the last event state and has no focus-loss clear. Hold `Shift` or `W`, switch away, and release the key while the window is unfocused. If the release is delivered elsewhere, DriveLab can retain rear view or throttle. Clear the key cache and release rear view on focus loss, and add a lifecycle test. `src/laboratory/drive_lab.gd:173–203, 550–560, 652–659`.

## Support and presentation boundaries

- **False ground is possible under the current sensor rule.** The default `0.75 m` reach starts at the middle of a `1.0 m` hull, so an upright hull can report grounded with **25 cm of clearance** and while rising. With the road outside range, a same-mask car roof or the top of a thin slab 0.6 m below the center can win the center ray and ground the car. A ceiling strictly above a downward ray start cannot be selected. With a tilted or interpenetrating pose, an upward-facing deck top below a ray start can qualify. The overpass fixture places the deck above the car and tests the first case only. Before motor integration, separate support candidates from committed ground, use collider and route eligibility plus clearance and relative motion, and retain hit identity so the caller can enforce those rules. `src/simulation/ground_support_probe.gd:93–128, 279–342, 351–393`; `tools/support_lab/support_lab_driver.gd:691–716, 900–943`.
- **Seams and banks have bounded coverage.** Sideways and inverted frames are rejected before queries. Ordinary matched 12° bank poses have a positive test. The default normal cutoff of `0.5` admits **60° slopes**, although its comment says 30°. This needs a documented threshold decision before adhesion. The center-miss fallback can ground on two same-side hits and pools all eligible footprint distances, so a third hit on a different level can veto a coherent pair. A legal center hit bypasses spread entirely. The fixtures establish the authored center gap and uneven-spread behavior; actual seam and edge policy belongs with adhesion. `src/simulation/ground_support_probe.gd:110–128, 244–254, 333–367`; `tools/support_lab/support_lab_driver.gd:421–447, 603–637, 783–820`.
- **Camera and HUD controls preserve simulation state in the inspected path.** DriveLab calls presentation after it installs the committed state and logs the tick. Camera actions change the rig and presentation counters. The HUD shows committed speed magnitude, labels backward travel `REVERSE` from signed forward speed, and always supplies `GRIP` because the lab has no drift-phase feed. A backward slide can therefore show `REVERSE` before reverse engages. The camera snaps at each committed update and keeps the forward-mode camera position when looking rearward. Those are interpretation and visual-feel limits. DriveLab also starts with `CarState.grounded = true` and holds the hull at fixed height, so its telemetry cannot establish support or landing. `src/laboratory/drive_lab.gd:286–319, 374–398, 534–544, 616–646`; `src/presentation/speed_hud.gd:235–255`; `src/presentation/camera_rig.gd:333–340, 403–424`; `src/simulation/car_state.gd:33`.
