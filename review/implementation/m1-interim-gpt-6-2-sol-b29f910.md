## Findings

1. **High, M1.2 — Ordinary road sections are separated by unintended gaps.** `src/laboratory/handling_lab_builder.gd:229–287` gives each new section its first sample one step beyond the boundary. `_split_runs()` then splits on each section or surface change without sharing the boundary sample (`:347–361`). Mesh and collision triangles only span consecutive samples within a run (`:386–417`, `:435–444`). At `gravel.entry_arc` → `gravel.sector`, this omits about 1 m of centerline coverage at the surface change (`src/laboratory/handling_lab_data.gd:454–463`). The same mechanism affects the bank and crest sequences and omits the first metre of the landing pad after the intentional jump gap. It also makes the restarted shoulder strips start a sample late. An integrated car could lose support or hit an exposed section face where the fixture intends a continuous transition. The validator checks the authored gravel length and selected ray hits, leaving this boundary coverage untested (`tools/handling_lab/validate_handling_lab.gd:338–344`, `:473–494`). Build each continuous section from its exact start cross-section and avoid internal end caps on joined road. Preserve actual jump gaps and give independently restarted strips their own start cross-section. Add Jolt probes immediately before, within, and after representative joins, plus checks for the intended jump gap and landing start.

2. **High, M1.1 — Reverse eligibility compares a signed velocity component with a positive speed limit.** `src/simulation/arcade_motor.gd:244–261` accepts `forward_speed <= reverse_trigger_speed_m_s`. A grounded car moving backward at 5 m/s qualifies against a 1 m/s trigger, as does a car sliding laterally at road speed with little forward motion. The coordinator observed reverse engaged after 20 committed ticks in the backward case. My source inspection confirms the cause. The design says reverse starts only below 1 m/s (`docs/GAME_DESIGN.md:11`), while the implementation documents an inclusive boundary (`src/simulation/arcade_motor.gd:38–42`). Gate new engagement using road-plane speed magnitude, resolve and test the exact threshold boundary, and preserve the behavior of an already-engaged reverse. Add backward, lateral, and interrupted-hold cases to the existing forward-speed regression (`tests/handling/pure_motor_entry.gd:824–870`). The airborne branch also preserves a partial hold through release and re-press, so include an interruption across airtime if the hold must be continuous (`src/simulation/arcade_motor.gd:241–265`, `:332–336`).

3. **Medium, M1.3 — Steering unwind can indefinitely mask a continuous low-speed exit.** Both entry and sustained phases reset the low-speed timer on every unwind tick, and reset the unwind timer on low-speed ticks with stronger steering (`src/simulation/drift_phase_model.gd:336–337`, `:358–404`). A car continuously below the exit speed can alternate centered and substantial steer without ever reaching either hold threshold. That can leave `SUSTAINED` at full influence even at standstill. The existing validator exercises unwind and low speed separately (`tools/drift_lab/validate_drift_lab.gd:451–516`, `:599–653`). Accumulate the two holds independently and select a deterministic transition reason when both mature. Test continuous low speed with oscillating steering.

4. **Low, M1.3 — A valid zero tap threshold silently disables drift entry through that control.** Tuning validation accepts zero for both tap thresholds (`src/simulation/drift_phase_model.gd:125–129`). The edge detector then requires a previous value strictly below zero (`:324–331`), which a normally sampled command cannot produce. Require a positive tap threshold, or define zero as a transition from neutral to any positive input. Cover both handbrake and Standard-assisted brake.

5. **Low, M1.5 — The camera accepts a finite singular pose and can install a collapsed camera basis.** `set_target_pose()` accepts a transform with all-zero basis vectors (`src/presentation/camera_rig.gd:181–195`, `:439–445`). Every fallback direction then remains zero, and the final guard checks only finiteness (`:388–432`). Rear view also uses the unnormalized target Z axis, so an ordinary scaled target supplies a non-unit camera axis to transform assignment and smoothing. Normalize the rear direction and validate the resulting camera basis before assignment or quaternion conversion. Add scaled-target and singular-pose cases and assert that rejection preserves the last usable camera transform.
