## Ranked findings

### 1. The shared `TacticalIntent` / observation contract is named but never defined, and its "freeze" is scheduled after the milestone that must first implement it — major

- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` §Architecture (baseline lines 19–25); `PLAN.md` M4.2 (line 108) and M4B.1 (line 119); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` §Content contracts (the contracts table, line 74 ff., defines Car/Track/Event/Upgrade/AI profile/Ghost/Model — but no intent or observation schema).
- **Problem:** Both opponent families are specified to emit the same `TacticalIntent` ("preferred lane/offset, target pace within the safe envelope, follow/pass commitment, and drift preference", line 20) into a shared safety layer and path follower, and the learned policy consumes the same observation contract — yet no document gives the field list, units, ranges, update rate, history length, or schema version for either. The observation feature list is explicitly deferred ("Define and version the exact feature list before training", line 25), and the only scheduled definition task, M4B.1 "Freeze the shared observation/intent contract", runs *after* M4.2, where the Classic planner must already produce that structure.
- **Concrete consequence:** the M4 implementer invents a schema. When M4B.1 freezes a different one, the Classic planner, safety layer, path follower, and the learned policy's action head all need rework, and M4's acceptance evidence (100 seeded races per difficulty, `PLAN.md` line 113) was measured against a contract that then changed. In the worst case the learned action space does not map onto the classic intent, undermining the fairness claim ("same low-level controller and physical limits") that the entire opponent design rests on.
- **Proposed correction:** add a versioned schema stub — field names, units, ranges, update rate, history length, backward-compatibility rules — to the `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` content-contracts table as an M0–M2 deliverable, exactly like the Car/Track/Event contracts. M4.2 implements to it; M4B.1 ratifies and versions it rather than creating it.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M4, M4B, T10 (AI legality), and the M4B inference-parity gate (≥10,000 held-out observations). Verify with a schema-validation test that rejects unversioned or out-of-range intents/observations, wired into CI at M0.

### 2. "Reference driver / reference pace" anchors at least four acceptance clauses but is never defined or assigned — minor

- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` difficulty table (lines 93–95) and line 97 ("relative to a documented reference driver"); `PLAN.md` M4 acceptance (line 113, "reference player paces"); `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` gates (lines 68–70, "matched Classic driver", "matched Classic baseline"); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` T11 (line 157, "reference human pace ranges").
- **Problem:** every pacing and fairness gate is measured against an artifact that no milestone owns or defines. Nothing says whether the reference is a scripted telemetry driver, a hand-tuned bot, or human runs, or how it is versioned.
- **Concrete consequence:** M4 can qualify Rookie at 85–90% of one baseline while M4B measures Surge's ±10% pace gate against a different one; the contact-rate comparison ("no worse than the matched Classic baseline by more than 10%") becomes unreproducible across milestones, and M8.7's closing evidence is not comparable to M4B's.
- **Proposed correction:** make the reference-pace artifact an explicit M4 deliverable — e.g., a versioned scripted reference driver with recorded per-course telemetry, plus a documented procedure for optional human reference runs — and require every pace/fairness gate to cite its revision.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M4.3, M4B, M8.1, M8.7, T10/T11; verification is the gate reports themselves once they cite a baseline revision.

### 3. The 60–95-second lap target is unreachable at its low end for the three longest courses under the specified top speeds — minor

- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 114 (lap target); course table lines 108–113 (Canyon Voltage ≈4.1 km at line 110, Stormpeak Finale ≈4.3 km at line 112); top-speed spec line 35 (205–235 km/h).
- **Problem (arithmetic on the document's own figures):** at the 235 km/h ceiling (≈65.3 m/s), a flat-out lap of Stormpeak cannot beat ~66 s and Canyon cannot beat ~63 s — before any cornering, braking, or the mandatory corner rhythm. A 60-second lap on those courses is physically unattainable without exceeding the specified top speed; even the 95 s high end on Stormpeak implies a ~163 km/h race average that only near-perfect driving reaches. The same tension, milder, applies to Metro Sunset (≈3.8 km → ~58 s floor).
- **Concrete consequence:** an implementer tuning Canyon or Stormpeak toward 60 s either quietly raises top speeds (breaking the "no exaggerated HUD numbers disconnected from the simulation" commitment, line 35) or burns M7/M8 tuning time chasing an impossible number, then ships whatever the real pace turns out to be with the target unmet.
- **Proposed correction:** replace the single band with per-course targets derived from the measured reference pace (finding 2's artifact) — keep 60–95 s only where course length allows, raise it for the 4.1–4.3 km courses — and keep the existing hedge that length is provisional.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M7.3, M7.5, M8.1; verification is a measured lap-time distribution per course against its stated target.

### 4. The 120 ms Expert reaction delay is finer than the 5 Hz (200 ms) tactical decision period it belongs to — minor

- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` difficulty table lines 93–95 vs `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 25 ("Run tactical decisions initially at 5 Hz … immediate collision/road safety at every 60 Hz simulation tick").
- **Problem:** tactical intents update every 200 ms, so a 120 ms reaction target is quantized to a single update cycle; Racer (200 ms) and Expert (120 ms) then differ by at most one cycle at the tactical layer, while the actually sub-200 ms reactions live in the per-tick hazard layer, which the difficulty table does not mention. The published numbers therefore do not describe the mechanism that will be implemented.
- **Concrete consequence:** difficulty tuning (M4.3) chases an 80 ms difference that the decision rate cannot express, and reviewers cannot tell from the table which layer a given delay refers to.
- **Proposed correction:** either implement and measure reaction latency against the 60 Hz follower/hazard layer and say so in the table, or quantize the published delays to the decision period (e.g., 400/200/200 ms) and document where they are applied.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M4.3, M4B; verification is scenario tests recording response-latency distributions per difficulty profile.

### 5. Handling numerics have no freeze point before the M7.7 learned-rival retraining, while M8.1 tunes car parity afterwards — minor

- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 51 ("A simulator/handling revision invalidates affected training/evaluation results and requires retraining or demonstrated compatibility"); `PLAN.md` M7.7 (line 166), M8.1 (line 174), M8.7.
- **Problem:** M7.7 trains and evaluates the shipped rivals, then M8.1 tunes car parity and drift-versus-grip — i.e., changes the very simulation the policies were trained in. Under the invalidation clause this silently voids the M7.7 evaluation and forces a retrain inside M8, after the M4B budget was already closed and reviewed.
- **Concrete consequence:** the M8.7 learned-AI gate reopens late in the project, or worse, closes on evaluation evidence produced against a superseded handling revision.
- **Proposed correction:** declare an explicit handling-numerics freeze at M7.7; route any later motor/handling change through the documented compatibility demonstration or a bounded retrain, and keep M8.7 open until re-evaluation against the shipped revision passes. Model cards should record the simulator revision they were evaluated against.
- **Affected milestones / verification:** M7.7, M8.1, M8.7; verification is the model-card revision chain plus the M8.7 gate results.

### 6. Drafting is fully specified in gameplay but absent from the simulation contract and test matrix — minor

- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Drafting (lines 49–51); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` motor state list (line 64), `DriverCommand` contract (line 56), T02 (line 148).
- **Problem:** nothing in the technical documents requires wake detection, the up-to-2% drag reduction, the 0.5 s fade, the no-stacking cap, or identical AI/player behavior to exist in the shared simulation, and no test row covers them.
- **Concrete consequence:** an implementation that omits drafting entirely violates no stated contract and passes T02 as written; the mechanic then appears late as a handling surprise (it interacts with the 3% top-speed upgrade cap and with the AI pace baselines from finding 2).

## Feasibility experiments (proposals only — not run during this review)

**E1. Headless bridge throughput and reset-semantics pilot.** Inputs: M0-pinned Godot 4.7.2 headless export, Riviera graybox, 8–16 parallel environments, batched 12-tick decision steps. Cost limit: ≤2 CPU-hours on one reference machine. Pass: ≥500 aggregate decision steps/s sustained, exact 12-tick advancement and complete reset verified (all participants, gates, timers, RNG, policy history, queued events). Decision: sets the PPO step/wall-time cap and confirms the curriculum can stay single-agent; failure triggers the plan's own rule (optimize batching and scenario length) before any compute increase. Resolves the M4B.2 gate and the "training too slow" risk row.

**E2. Drift-versus-grip parity benchmark.** Inputs: M1 benchmark sweeper, the three starter configurations, assisted-driver captures. Cost limit: ≤1 day of tuning iteration. Pass: a clean drift within 5% of the effective grip line, repeatable 12–40° slips, no uncontrolled oscillation at extreme steering/countersteer. Decision: confirms the drift model is worth building content on before M2 authoring; failure sends handling back to redesign rather than course expansion. Resolves the M1 acceptance gate and the "drifting is stable but uninteresting" risk row.

**E3. Fixed-tick invariance and maximum-speed collision integrity.** Inputs: M1 laboratory, rendered-FPS sweep at 30/60/120, ~65 m/s barrier/wall/car-contact, crest, jump, and landing fixtures. Cost limit: hours. Pass: benchmark results within the documented tolerance at all three rendered rates; no tunneling, launching, or nonfinite states. Decision: validates the custom-motor/collision architecture before M2 bakes collision from it (T09 depends on collision integrity). Failure triggers bounded movement subdivisions or a tick-rate decision, measured first.

**E4. Four-camera stress measurement and renderer selection.** Inputs: a representative worst-case scene (eight cars, dense scenery, all split layouts) at 1920×1080 on the recorded M1-class and GTX 1650-class reference machines, Compatibility vs Mobile rendering. Cost limit: ≤1 day. Pass: p95 ≤16.7 ms / p99 ≤25 ms in every layout at default settings with the chosen renderer. Decision: locks the renderer and the documented rendering budget before bulk asset production, exactly as M3.5 requires. Failure reduces scenery/resolution cost rather than simulation rate.

**E5. BC→PPO behavioral-distinction pilot on Riviera.** Inputs: frozen Classic-driver demonstrations, a capped PPO pilot (≤2M decision steps, ≤8 wall-clock hours, ≥3 seeds), held-out evaluation seeds disjoint from training. Cost limit: as stated, hard-capped. Pass (pre-registered): Classic–Surge, Classic–Switch, Surge–Switch each show ≥25% meaningful differences over ≥200 matched opportunity windows across ≥6 scenario families; safety interventions <1% emergency / <5% material; completion ≥99% with deadlines disabled. Decision: confirms the learned family can meet its gates; one bounded revision/retrain is allowed, and persistent failure is a scope decision for the owner — the plan already forbids relabeling a scripted personality as learned AI, which this review endorses.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- **Fixed-tick shared simulation strictly separated from cameras, audio, and UI** (delivery principle 3; the runtime-ownership table in `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`). This is what makes race results camera-independent, four-player fair, and tests repeatable — and it is the reason the T03 frame-rate-invariance gate is meaningful.
- **Gating handling, the course pipeline, and four-camera cost before bulk content** (M1–M3 order; delivery principle 2; the risk table's early-evidence column). The expensive unknowns are settled while they are still cheap to fix.
- **A tiny MLP over a shared low-level controller, with frozen Classic opponents and a single-agent PPO curriculum.** Fairness is structural (same motor, same limits, no catch-up), training stays tractable on local hardware, and the weights-only export with GDScript inference and parity tests keeps the runtime free of Python/PyTorch/ONNX/cloud dependencies — which the offline, public-domain release goals require.
- **The manifest/provenance contract with a hard release rights gate**, including the external-asset exception policy (item-level CC0 evidence only) and the refusal to let unknown legal origins ship. This is what makes the public-domain claim auditable instead of aspirational.
- **Honest unverified-status handling** — open gates for unavailable hardware, unsigned-build labeling when signing credentials are absent, and explicit reporting when no independent human playtest has occurred. This prevents the most common failure mode of agent-produced plans: claiming completion from bot evidence.
- **Choosing the renderer from measurements rather than API-age assumptions**, and the explicit note that a successful export does not verify a platform. Both are correct and worth keeping verbatim.
