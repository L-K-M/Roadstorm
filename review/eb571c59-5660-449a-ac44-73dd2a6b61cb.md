## Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.** The plan is unusually complete: it defines the driving model, race rules, modes, economy, AI families, content pipeline, performance budgets, verification matrix, and licensing policy in enough detail that an unfamiliar agent could build the intended game. My confidence that the documents are sufficient is high once the findings below are addressed. Confidence is moderate on the required learned-Roadstormer behavioral goal: the approach is reasonable and correctly gated, but the two-style differentiation requirement is the largest open feasibility risk and needs a bounded pilot before bulk work.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Four-player performance target and geometry budget contradict each other

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`, Platform and rendering baseline (line 11) and Performance budgets (lines 130-133); `PLAN.md`, M3 acceptance (line 101) and M3.5 (line 99).
- **Problem:** The plan targets 1920×1080 at 60 FPS for one *and* four players. The geometry gate is stated as "≤350k visible triangles/250 draw calls in one view" and "≤900k triangles/650 draw calls aggregated across four views." If four active viewports each render the same scene, the single-view cap permits about 1.4M triangles and 1,000 draw calls summed, which exceeds the aggregate cap. The aggregate cap therefore silently requires roughly a 35% per-view reduction in four-player, but no document says so. "Visible triangles" is also undefined: it is unclear whether frustum culling, LOD selection, shadow-pass geometry, or transparency overdraw are included.
- **Failure example:** M3's representative-scenery stress scene passes at 350k triangles per view because content was authored to that number; four real players on a dense section then exceed the 900k aggregate, forcing late prop/shadow cuts on courses already authored, or a missed 60 FPS gate on the stated M1/GTX-1650-class reference hardware.
- **Proposed correction:** State the four-player budget per active viewport (for example, average ≤225k triangles and ≤162 draw calls per view under a 4-way split) or explicitly require player-count-dependent prop density, LOD, and shadow scaling. Define the measurement method (camera frustum, LOD level, shadow pass inclusion, GPU timer). Require the M3.5 worst-case scene to use all four cameras, eight cars, and the densest authored section with shadows enabled.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M0.6, M3.5, M7.6, M8.3. Verify with a four-SubViewport capture on each recorded reference machine; record frame-time distribution and draw calls per view.

### 2. Car dimensions are unspecified while road width, grid layout, passing clearance, and AI safety metrics depend on them

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 57 (eight participants), line 89 ("one vehicle width plus a safety margin"), line 114 (road widths 9-20 m, 8 m squeezes); `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 80 (content contract requires "dimensions") and line 80's clearance discussion; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 79 (safety-intervention threshold defined as "more than 0.1 car width").
- **Problem:** No target overall length, width, height, wheelbase, or contact-proxy size is given for any of the twelve cars. Many numerical rules nevertheless assume a known car size: an eight-car grid on a 9-20 m road, 8 m two-car squeezes, side clearance of one vehicle width plus margin, tunnel/bridge/jump clearance for the tallest car, and the 0.1-car-width intervention metric.
- **Failure example:** M2 authors an 8 m squeeze assuming 1.8 m wide cars; M1 later builds a 2.1 m wide car and the squeeze becomes a one-car bottleneck, violating "narrow sections do not force unavoidable collisions" and invalidating the "0.1 car width" safety statistics. The grid start may also not fit eight cars across the widest permitted road.
- **Proposed correction:** Add dimension classes and target ranges to `GAME_DESIGN.md` and `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` before course authoring (for example, length 3.6-4.8 m, width 1.7-2.0 m, height 1.2-1.5 m, documented per-car variation band). Derive minimum road width, grid spacing, and side-clearance values from the widest/tallest car, and bind the AI safety-intervention threshold to that documented width.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M1.1/M1.4, M2.2/M2.3, M4.4, M7.6. Verify with an eight-car standing start at maximum car width, a two-wide pass through the narrowest section, and clearance tests for tunnels, bridges, and jumps.

### 3. Learned Roadstormers: under-specified training contract, no stop rule, and a BC-to-PPO path that risks duplicating Classic behavior

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 25-31 (hierarchical controller), 37-45 (imitation then PPO), 61 (M4B placement), 71 (behavioral-distinction gate), 77 (interpretation); `PLAN.md` M4B.1-M4B.7 (lines 119-126) and Risks table (lines 222-223).
- **Problem:** The plan mandates freezing the observation/intent contract before training, but the contract itself is absent: no feature vector, action-head semantics, history length, normalization scheme, or style conditioning is specified. There is no numerical convergence criterion, no fixed step/wall-clock cap for the learning pilot, no explicit divergence check between the behavioral-cloning result and the RL result, and no fallback that preserves the two-style requirement if PPO fails. The style mechanism is ambiguous ("a personality input or two independently trained checkpoints"). The ≥25% "meaningful differences" gate is defined only at final evaluation, and "meaningful" is not operationally defined until then.
- **Failure example:** M4B initializes from Classic-driver demonstrations, then PPO with limited exploration converges back toward those demonstrations. Surge and Switch differ only slightly, the 200-window comparison shows both are indistinguishable from Classic, and the milestone cannot close. Because the plan forbids relabeling scripted personalities as learned AI, the schedule slips with no compliant way to preserve the requested scope.
- **Proposed correction:** Pre-register the observation and `TacticalIntent` schemas, including dimensions, normalization, history length, and per-style reward offsets, before M4B.2. Add a hard pilot stop rule combining a step cap, a wall-clock cap, and a minimum held-out divergence metric. Add an explicit BC-versus-RL divergence test so an inert copy is detected early rather than at final evaluation. Choose and document one style mechanism (a single conditioned policy is easier to keep within the parameter budget and parity requirements than two separately trained checkpoints). Define "meaningful difference" as pre-declared decision-category deltas measured during the pilot. Add a contingency that still satisfies the no-relabel rule, such as stylizing through curated demonstration sub-mixes plus RL fine-tuning.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4B.1-M4B.7, M5.7, M7.7, M8.7. Verify with the 10k-100k-step throughput pilot, the ≥10,000-observation inference-parity test, ≥200 matched windows per pair, and held-out races on reserved layouts.

### 4. Public-domain dedications lack a named rights holder for agent-authored output

- **Severity:** major.
- **File and section:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` lines 7, 13, 22, 28-30, 112-115, 125; `LICENSE`; `README.md` license section.
- **Problem:** The plan dedicates original code under the Unlicense and original art/audio/models under CC0, records agent/provider/model/date provenance, and states that licensing can dedicate only rights the contributor holds, while also stating that an agent must not sign as a person or claim rights on a person's behalf. It never identifies who the rights holder is for agent-generated material. Provider/account terms are required for optional remote image/audio/model generation (line 103) but not for the agents producing the baseline itself. Release gate T15 checks notices, manifests, and package contents, not dedication authority.
- **Failure example:** At release, every asset traces to "LLM-generated, provider X," but no assignment or authority record exists. If provider X's terms leave output rights with the provider or are silent, the owner's Unlicense/CC0 dedication may be ineffective, and downstream users cannot rely on the public-domain claim even though the notices are present and correct.
- **Proposed correction:** Add a mandatory provenance field for "rights holder/contributor of record" plus the legal basis (account ownership, assignment, or applicable provider terms) for every asset family. Record provider/account terms for every generation service used, including local agent runs. Make the release gate verify the dedicating party's authority before shipping. Keep the existing "to the extent rights can be waived" hedge in the public statement.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M0.3, M8.6, M9.4; T15. Verify with a provenance audit covering 100% of shipped creative assets and code, each mapped to a named, documented rights holder.

### 5. Local DNF and withdrawal rules contradict between mode descriptions

- **Severity:** minor.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 168 versus line 170.
- **Problem:** Line 168 says "Local exhibition may advance after DNF normally." Line 170 says "Local Cup fixes its human slots at the start; a withdrawal converts that participant to AI for the remainder rather than admitting a new mid-cup participant." A reader cannot determine whether Local Cup counts as local exhibition, whether a DNF (as distinct from a withdrawal) advances, or how points and standings treat a converted slot.
- **Failure example:** One implementer lets a DNF'd Local Cup player continue as a human in later events; another converts the slot to AI after the first event. Both claim compliance, and results diverge for the same inputs.
- **Proposed correction:** State the DNF and withdrawal rule per mode (Quick Race exhibition, Local Cup, solo Career): whether the event continues, whether the slot persists, and how points are awarded.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M4.1, M6.1, T06. Verify with scenario tests for DNF and withdrawal in each mode.

### 6. Arcade Rush extension mechanics are undefined

- **Severity:** minor.
- **File and section:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 79.
- **Problem:** Time allowances are defined as percentages of a measured reference run, but the number of extensions, the trigger that earns one, the seconds added per extension, and the interaction with the "earned once in order" rule are not specified.
- **Failure example:** One implementer grants an extension at each of three intermediate gates; another grants one per lap. Expert's 102% allowance becomes either trivial or impossible, and the requirement that "Rookie must tolerate a brief mistake" cannot be verified.
- **Proposed correction:** Specify per-course extension count, trigger, added time, expiry behavior, and store it in the event definition. Validate with scripted stock-starter runs at all three difficulties against the measured reference.
- **Affected milestones and verification:** M6.1, M7.5, T06. Verify with scripted Arcade Rush runs and stored best times.

## Feasibility experiments

These are proposals for the implementing team, not actions taken during this review.

1. **Four-viewport render-cost pilot.** Inputs: a graybox Riviera section, eight simple car proxies, representative props, Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility renderer. Cost limit: one engineer-day on the recorded M1 8 GB and GTX-1650-class references. Pass/fail: four active SubViewports at 1920×1080 total sustain p95 ≤16.7 ms with the intended aggregate triangle/draw-call budget, including the directional shadow pass. Decision resolved: lock renderer and four-player content density before M2 bulk generation.
2. **Eight-car grid and narrow-section contact pilot.** Inputs: one agreed maximum car dimension set, a graybox straight, an 8 m squeeze, and the custom contact resolver. Cost limit: within M1. Pass/fail: no tunneling at 65 m/s, no vertical launch, a two-wide pass completes without forced contact, and the grid fits eight cars. Decision resolved: finalize car dimensions and minimum road widths before course authoring.
3. **Training-throughput pilot.** Inputs: headless Godot bridge, 1/8/16/32 parallel environments, 10k-100k decision steps. Cost limit: two hours wall-clock and 1M steps. Pass/fail: correct reset and 12-tick-per-decision semantics, plus a sustained step rate that makes a PPO pilot feasible within the M4B budget. Decision resolved: continue with Godot-as-environment or add a validated surrogate environment and batch/offline training.
4. **Style-separation micro-study.** Inputs: a behavioral-cloning policy trained on Classic demonstrations, two RL fine-tunes with distinct risk/patience reward offsets, and 200 matched opportunity windows. Cost limit: one bounded pilot run per style. Pass/fail: ≥25% pre-defined meaningful differences for each pair and measurable divergence from the BC prior. Decision resolved: proceed to full training or redesign the two-style mechanism and curriculum.
5. **License/provenance audit dry run.** Inputs: a small sample of generated assets, one model card, and the draft manifest. Cost limit: half a day. Pass/fail: every item traces to a source recipe, content hash, and a named rights holder with provider/account terms. Decision resolved: whether T15 as written is sufficient to support the public-domain release claim.

## Cross-document consistency

- The single-view and four-view geometry caps in `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` cannot both hold without an unstated per-view reduction (Finding 1).
- Vehicle dimensions are required by the technical content contract and assumed by game-design passing/clearance rules and by the AI safety metric, but are defined nowhere (Finding 2).
- The local DNF/withdrawal rule differs between `GAME_DESIGN.md` line 168 and line 170 (Finding 5).
- The two-style mechanism is described as either a personality input or two checkpoints (`OPPONENT_AI.md` line 27) without choosing one; this interacts with the training and parity gates (Finding 3).
- The Arcade Rush extension rule is missing while its difficulty targets are numeric (Finding 6).
- I found no contradiction among the Roadstormer gates themselves: `PLAN.md` M4B, `OPPONENT_AI.md` acceptance gates, and `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` T10-T12 agree on offline inference, parity, safety, completion, and held-out evaluation.
- The economy arithmetic is internally consistent: 800 + 1,600 = 2,400 per category, ×3 = 7,200; the base reward equals one level-1 upgrade; eight finishes is an approximation that assumes better-than-last-place and some drift rewards (see Optional improvements).

## Optional improvements

These are not required for the verdict.

- Add an explicit four-player content-density strategy (per-view prop caps, shadow scaling) rather than relying only on global budgets. Presentation-only; no requested-feature change.
- Estimate the endurance-testing compute for T11 (100 seeds × 6 tracks × 3 difficulties = 1,800 races) and document headless parallelization and sampling with confidence bounds. No scope change.
- Make the experiential-review protocol concrete: predefined questions for handling readability, rival fairness, and audio comfort, using captured footage and any available human playtest, recorded separately from automatic checks. The plan already requires experiential review; this only sharpens it.
- Specify spectator behavior when more than one human finishes before a race ends in split-screen; the current text covers a single finished human.
- Clarify the cup tie-break ("earlier event finishes from latest to earliest") with a worked example to prevent divergent implementations.
- Economy tuning note: "about eight ordinary standard finishes" for a full upgrade assumes better than eighth place plus some drift rewards; eight last-place finishes earn 6,400 credits and need nine. State the assumed finish position or drift rate, or tune the base reward if eight finishes at the back is the intended floor.
- If the training pilot shows Godot stepping dominates cost, consider a lightweight validated surrogate tactical environment for PPO with Godot reserved for final evaluation. This adds development tooling only; it does not change player-facing scope.

Scope-changing recommendations: none of the required corrections above removes or adds a requested player-facing feature. The only changes that could affect perceived scope are (a) reducing four-player prop density or shadow quality if the render pilot fails, which is a presentation trade-off to preserve the requested four-player mode, and (b) the Roadstormer contingency in Finding 3, which would change how the two learned styles are produced but not the requested outcome. Both should be recorded as explicit scope decisions if adopted.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- Fixed-tick shared simulation fully isolated from cameras, audio, UI, and scenery animation, with an explicit rule that audiovisual changes cannot alter race results.
- One shared `World3D` with per-player `SubViewport`/`Camera3D`/HUD overlays and no duplicated per-camera simulation.
- Custom arcade motor on `CharacterBody3D` with world sweeps, ground probes, and a central `ContactResolver` that owns car-to-car response while physics masks exclude ordinary car-car response.
- Decision-gated milestones with M1/M3/M4B/M5 gates and a rule against expanding content before handling, pipeline, and four-camera costs pass.
- Hierarchical AI: shared 60 Hz follower and immediate safety, 5 Hz tactics, small MLP, offline inference with GDScript parity testing, pre-declared safety interventions, held-out evaluation, and a Classic fallback that explicitly does not count as success.
- Honest verification culture: untested platforms stay unverified, bot races do not prove enjoyment, and automated checks are separated from experiential review.
- Licensing hygiene: engine and dependency notices kept separate from original material, external creative assets accepted only with explicit item-level CC0 evidence, provenance manifest with hashes and regeneration commands.
- Save idempotency with event-instance transaction IDs, atomic writes, backups, and versioned ghosts/records.
- Course compiler producing render, collision, AI samples, gates, anchors, and minimap from one authored source, with validation that intentionally rejects broken fixtures.
