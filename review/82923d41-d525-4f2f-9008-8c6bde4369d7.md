## Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.**

Confidence: high. The document set is unusually disciplined for a plan at this stage — it separates simulation from presentation, names what each component must *not* own, defines acceptance gates that are mostly measurable, and is honest about what it has not verified. An unfamiliar implementation agent could start M0 tomorrow and would not have to invent essential rules.

However, nine findings below would each cause avoidable rework or silent quality failure if left as written, and three of them (1, 5, 9) are missing *measurement definitions* rather than missing design: the plan repeatedly requires tuning "relative to a documented reference" without ever documenting the reference. That is the single most common way this class of plan produces two agents who build incompatible systems. None of the findings is a blocker to starting work, and none is a feasibility risk that I would call unresolved — with the caveat below.

The one item that *could* become an unresolved feasibility risk is the Roadstormer behavioural-difference gate (finding 6). The plan already treats it as a decision gate with a correct fallback policy, but it currently sits on the critical path to all content production without a time box. That is a scheduling decision, not a technical unknown.

## Ranked findings

### 1. The lap-time band and the top-speed band are jointly infeasible at the fast end for four of six courses

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Courses, line 114 ("Target 60–95-second laps after measurement and adjustment") against §Speed and surfaces, line 35 ("Target stock top speeds of approximately 205–235 km/h") and the per-course lengths in the course table, lines 107–112.
- **Problem.** A 60-second lap requires an average speed of `length / 60`. Computed from the document's own numbers:

  | Course | Length | Avg speed for a 60 s lap | Avg speed for a 95 s lap |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Emerald Rally | 3.5 km | 210 km/h | 133 km/h |
  | Riviera Run | 3.6 km | 216 km/h | 136 km/h |
  | Metro Sunset | 3.8 km | 228 km/h | 144 km/h |
  | Alpine Switchback | 3.9 km | 234 km/h | 148 km/h |
  | Canyon Voltage | 4.1 km | **246 km/h** | 155 km/h |
  | Stormpeak Finale | 4.3 km | **258 km/h** | 163 km/h |

  Canyon Voltage and Stormpeak Finale cannot reach a 60-second lap at all without exceeding the specified 205–235 km/h top-speed ceiling. The other four require 210–234 km/h *average*, which for courses described as having a "village squeeze", a "narrow traverse" and a "dam climb" means driving essentially flat out with no braking at all.
- **Failure example.** An agent authoring Stormpeak Finale to hit a 60-second lap either raises its top speed above 235 km/h (breaking the §Speed and surfaces commitment and the "rather than using exaggerated HUD numbers disconnected from the simulation" requirement in the same paragraph) or shortens the course below 4.3 km (breaking the course table). Two agents following the documents literally will produce two incompatible courses, and the difference will only surface at M7.6's full car/course matrix.
- **Proposed correction.** Replace the single 60–95 s band with a per-course target derived from a measured reference run, and add a hard feasibility rule to the track contract: `required average speed = length / target lap time` must be at most a stated fraction (suggest 0.80) of the slowest stock car's measured top speed on that course. Keep 60–95 s as the *distribution* of resulting targets, not as a per-course target. The document already concedes "Course length is provisional; road rhythm and race duration take priority over hitting an exact distance" — this makes that concession executable instead of aspirational.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M2 (route authoring), M7.6 (full car/course matrix), M8.1 (tuning). Add a validation step to the M2.3 track validator that fails a route whose required average speed exceeds the cap; the existing T09 track-bake gate is the natural home.

### 2. Split-screen audio never decides which listener the engine uses

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` §Split-screen and audio, lines 104–112.
- **Problem.** The plan correctly requires "one music/UI mix and an original shared sound-priority system, rather than four full independent listener mixes" — but it never states which listener transform the engine will use. Verified against the Godot 4.7 class reference: `AudioListener3D` overrides the location sounds are heard from and is enabled with `make_current()`, and there is exactly one current listener. With four `SubViewport`s each holding a `Camera3D`, there is no automatic per-viewport listener; the mix position is whichever listener happens to be current, which is an implementation-defined outcome the plan leaves open.
- **Failure example.** A four-player race where engine and tire panning and attenuation follow only player 1's camera (or jump between panels as viewports are enabled and disabled), so players 2–4 hear a mix centred on a car they do not control. This is discovered during M3.4 ("shared audio mixing") and forces a rework of the audio director *after* M5.4's "balanced four-player mix" has already been tuned against the wrong reference.
- **Proposed correction.** Add one explicit contract line to the technical design: exactly one `AudioListener3D` exists in the race scene; name its owner (recommended: the host slot, or a documented weighted centre of the local cars); require every other camera's listener to be disabled; and require per-player relevance to be computed manually from each player's own camera position rather than relying on engine panning. This is consistent with the plan's existing intent — it just needs to be stated as a rule instead of left to the implementer.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M3.4, M5.4, M8.2. Verify with a four-viewport scene containing engine sources at four distinct world positions, asserting a single current listener at the documented transform, plus captured per-player gain values.

### 3. The 32-voice budget cannot cover the specified worst-case mix

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` §Split-screen and audio, line 110 ("Start with a 32-voice budget and four engine families") against `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` §Effects and engine sound, lines 77–79, and `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Experience review checklist, line 213.
- **Problem.** The required inventory is four engine families each with idle/load/coast loops and crossfades, roughly 40 reusable tire/contact/UI/environment layers, plus music. A conservative worst case with four players and eight cars:

  | Source | Voices |
  | --- | --- |
  | Four local cars, two active crossfaded engine layers each | 8 |
  | Up to four nearby rivals, one layer each | 4 |
  | Tire layers for eight cars (asphalt scrub + sustained slip) | 16 |
  | Music | 1 |
  | Environment layers audible at once (surf, wind, waterfall, aircraft/turbine/train) | ≥5 |
  | **Total** | **≈34** |

  That is above the 32-voice cap before a single impact, landing, or UI cue.
- **Failure example.** On Stormpeak Finale with four players, the shared priority system must drop either a player's own engine layer or the turbine/waterfall bed to stay under 32 voices. The experience checklist item "Sound remains intelligible and comfortable with four engines, environmental loops, and music" then fails even though every individual asset passed its −1 dBTP / LUFS gate. The plan hedges with "Start with a 32-voice budget... profile decoder/voice cost", but an implementer needs an allocation to implement against, not just a cap.
- **Proposed correction.** Either raise the initial budget with a measured justification, or publish a per-category voice allocation (for example engines 12, tires 8, environment 6, music 1, UI/cues 5) so the priority system has a defined contract. Then measure the true peak in M3.4/M8.3 rather than assuming it.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M3.4, M5.4, M8.3. Verify with a stress scene counting simultaneous voices at peak (four players, eight cars, the fullest environment kit) against the allocation table.

### 4. The AI reaction-delay table cannot be expressed at the specified tactical rate

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` §Architecture, line 25 ("Run tactical decisions initially at 5 Hz") against `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Fair and assertive opponents, lines 93–95 (reaction delays of ~300 ms, ~200 ms, ~120 ms).
- **Problem.** Neither document says where the reaction delay is applied. If it is applied at the tactical layer, 5 Hz gives 200 ms granularity, so Expert's 120 ms quantises to either 0 ms or 200 ms — a 200 ms error in the single most difficulty-sensitive parameter in the game.
- **Failure example.** An implementer delays the tactical intent by 200 ms for all three difficulties because that is the nearest 5 Hz multiple. Expert AI then reacts as fast as a perfect driver and Rookie AI reacts 100 ms slower than specified. T10's fairness check ("no hidden catch-up", "shared physical limits") passes in both cases, because nothing in it measures reaction latency — so the difficulty curve is silently wrong through M4.3 and M8.1.
- **Proposed correction.** State explicitly that reaction delay is a command-level delay line applied to the observation stream inside the 60 Hz follower, and that the 5 Hz tactical rate is independent of it. Add a T10 sub-check that measures the distribution of time from hazard onset to first braking command per difficulty and asserts the mean against the table.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M4.2, M4.3, M8.1. Verify with a scripted-hazard scenario (a car braking hard ahead, a late apex) measuring first-response latency per difficulty profile.

### 5. The "documented reference driver" that every difficulty percentage is relative to is never defined

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Fair and assertive opponents, lines 93–99.
- **Problem.** Rookie is "85–90% of validated safe reference pace", Racer "93–98%", Expert "100–103% reference pace where physically achievable". The reference driver is named but never defined — no car, no upgrade preset, no input source, no aggregation, no recording. Worse, the same paragraph states the percentages "apply to desired line/speed planning relative to a documented reference driver, never to physical car capability", which makes the reference a *planning* pace rather than a lap time. A planning pace cannot be measured by timing laps, so there is no measurement procedure at all.
- **Failure example.** Two agents implement Expert at "100–103%" differently: one as a 103% multiplier on the AI's own achievable cornering speed (invisible to the player), the other as a target lap time 3% faster than a recorded human run. Both pass T10's fairness checks. The two builds have completely different difficulty curves, and the M4 acceptance criterion "no route point repeatedly requires a recovery" is evaluated against two different baselines, so it cannot be compared across builds.
- **Proposed correction.** Define the reference as a reproducible artefact: a named Classic AI profile on a named stock car and course, with per-corner target speeds and lap times recorded into the course bundle together with its content revision. State that difficulty scales the *planning targets* derived from that artefact, record the derivation formula, and require T10 to check the derivation rather than the outcome.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M4.3, M8.1, T10, T11. Verify by regenerating the difficulty tables from the recorded reference and diffing against the shipped values.

### 6. The Roadstormer behavioural gates are jointly tight and gate all content production

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` §Implementation and acceptance, line 71, against `PLAN.md` M5 (lines 129–141) and M7 (lines 156–168).
- **Problem.** The behavioural-difference gate requires ≥25% meaningful differences for all three pairs (Classic–Surge, Classic–Switch, Surge–Switch) over ≥200 matched opportunity windows each, while *simultaneously* requiring clean mixed-course lap pace within ~10% of the matched Classic driver, heavy-contact rate no worse than baseline +10%, ≥99% AI-entry completion, emergency safety interventions <1%, and material corrections <5%. These are jointly achievable but not easy, and M5's acceptance requires both styles demonstrated in the slice. Because M7 depends on M5, the highest-uncertainty research item in the project gates all six courses and nine remaining cars.
- **Failure example.** Surge trains to within 8% of baseline pace but differs from Classic in only 18% of commit/wait windows. The gate fails, M5 cannot close, and M7's content production is blocked behind a retraining loop with no time box and no defined exit.
- **Proposed correction (changes scope timing, not scope content).** Roadstormers remain required for 1.0. Split the gate: let M5 close on one validated style in the slice plus a *measured* training pilot for the second, and move the full three-pair distinction gate to M8.7, where the complete learned-AI gate already lives. Alternatively, start the second style's training pilot in parallel with M6 so it lands before M7. Either way, put a wall-clock cap on the M4B retraining loop (the pilot measurement in experiment E4 supplies the number) and state the decision rule in advance.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M4B, M5, M7, M8.7. Verification is the existing gate table, run exactly as specified, with pass/fail recorded against the model card.

### 7. The font plan cannot satisfy the 150% text-scaling requirement

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` §Geometry and graphics workflow, line 71 ("Create an original readable geometric font or glyph atlas") against `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Local multiplayer interface, line 192 ("target at least 20-pixel essential labels and 32-pixel speed numerals within each quadrant, scalable to 150%").
- **Problem.** A raster glyph atlas cannot be scaled to 150% without resampling artefacts. Verified against the Godot font documentation: the scalable path is a dynamic font in TTF/OTF/WOFF/WOFF2. The plan's "font **or** glyph atlas" phrasing permits the choice that breaks the accessibility requirement.
- **Failure example.** The generator emits a 64 px atlas; the accessibility text-scale setting upscales it 1.5×, producing visibly soft HUD speed numerals — on the one setting whose entire purpose is readability. Discovered during M5.5's "accessible readability settings", after the font generator is already built.
- **Proposed correction.** Require the font generator to emit a real TTF/OTF (fontTools or an equivalent original script — MIT, and it must enter the development dependency inventory), with the atlas permitted only as a derived preview. Additionally, explicitly ban `SystemFont` resources: they would make rendering depend on fonts installed on the player's machine, which conflicts with the plan's reproducibility and readability goals and is currently unmentioned.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M5.5, M7.4 (UI and type), M8.2. Verify by rendering every HUD and menu string at 100/125/150% and diffing against a native-resolution render, alongside the missing-glyph check the plan already requires.

### 8. Trademark clearance is a release blocker with no scheduled task

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` §License scope, line 22 ("CC0 does not resolve trademark... the chosen product/car names remain working names until appropriately checked") and `PLAN.md` M9.6, line 193.
- **Problem.** Both documents correctly state that public-domain dedication is not trademark clearance, and M9.6 asks the owner to "Confirm the final product title and release destination with the owner if those have changed". But no milestone task performs or schedules the clearance, and M9.6 is conditional ("if those have changed"), so a conflict can be discovered after M7 has produced six courses and twelve named cars and M8 has balanced them.
- **Failure example.** A conflicting mark on the title or a car name is found at M9.6. The release is blocked, there is no fallback title, and renaming twelve cars plus every reference to them in courses, cups, HUD, saves and documentation is unbudgeted.
- **Proposed correction (changes scope).** Add a bounded clearance task covering the title and the twelve car names, with a recorded decision and at least one pre-approved fallback title. Best placed in M0 alongside the dependency inventory; at the latest, in M5 before bulk content. The cost is small and the downside is a blocked release.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M0 (recommended) or M5, then M7, M9. Verification: a recorded search and owner decision in `docs/`, listing the chosen title, the fallback, and the date.

### 9. Arcade Rush timing has no defined reference driver, and its Expert margin is below run-to-run variance

- **Severity:** major
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Mode definitions, line 79.
- **Problem.** "For each course, measure a clean stock starter run first. Set initial allowance and extensions so total available time is about 120% of that reference on Rookie, 110% on Racer, and 102% on Expert." Three things are undefined: who drives the reference run (a Classic AI? a scripted bot? a human capture?), how many runs and how they are aggregated (mean, median, best), and what triggers re-measurement when handling or course revisions change. Separately, 2% of a ~75-second lap is about 1.5 seconds — smaller than the cost of one slightly wide corner or one missed extension — so Expert is a perfect-run demand with no mistake tolerance, in a document that elsewhere insists on "forgiving recovery space".
- **Failure example.** Two agents measure the reference with different drivers and set allowances four seconds apart; a player who finishes both courses' Expert timers on one build fails on the other. Or a handling revision in M8.1 shifts lap times 3% and silently invalidates every Arcade Rush allowance, with nothing flagging it.
- **Proposed correction.** Define the reference as a reproducible artefact: a seeded Classic Racer AI run on a stock starter, median of five runs, recorded with its seed and content revision in the course bundle. Require allowances to be regenerated and re-validated whenever the handling or course revision changes, and set a stated minimum absolute margin (suggest no less than 3 seconds) so Expert stays playable at short lap times.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M6.1, M7.5 (per-course Arcade Rush timing), M8.1. Verify with a test case that recomputes allowances from the recorded reference and fails if the shipped values differ.

### 10. "Tires gives +5% lateral response/recovery rate" is undefined and can violate the plan's own drift-identity constraint

- **Severity:** minor
- **Location:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Upgrades, line 153.
- **Problem.** "Lateral response/recovery rate" is not tied to a named parameter. It could mean lateral-force response, the ~0.35 s drift-exit recovery blend from §Handling and drift, or reset recovery. The same paragraph warns that "Tires must not prevent drifting or silently turn a drift car into a grip car" and to "retune any upgrade that harms the intended handling identity" — but gives an implementer nothing to check against.
- **Failure example.** One agent implements Tires as a 5% per-level reduction in drift-exit recovery time. Slidewinder (drift score 5) at Tires 2 recovers from slides 10% faster, which is precisely the "drift car turned into a grip car" failure the document forbids. T02's boundary tests ("no invalid state, no weave/donut/wall farming") do not catch it, because the behaviour is legal, just wrong.
- **Proposed correction.** Name the parameter and its unit (for example the lateral force response time constant, in seconds, with a stated range), state the direction and the cap, and add an M8.1 check that each car's measured drift entry/sustain/exit behaviour at maximum Tires stays inside its stock character band. M1.6's "handling comparison report using the three starter configurations" is the natural template.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M6.2, M8.1. Verification: per-car handling comparison at stock versus maximum upgrades.

### 11. The economy acceptance criterion "roughly eight typical standard finishes" is not measurable

- **Severity:** minor
- **Location:** `PLAN.md` M6 acceptance, line 154, against `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` §Career and rewards, line 174.
- **Problem.** A standard three-lap finish pays 800 base + 400/300/200/100 for places 1–4 + up to 100 for drifting, i.e. 800–1300 credits. Fully upgrading one car costs 7,200. Computed from those numbers, the finishes needed range from 5.5 (repeated first place with maximum drift) to 9.0 (repeated eighth place with none). "Roughly eight" sits inside that range, but nothing defines "typical", so the gate cannot be evaluated — and M6.6's economy simulation has no stated reference distribution to run against.
- **Proposed correction.** Define the reference explicitly, for example the mean over a uniform 1st–8th place distribution with the drift bonus at 50%, and state the required range (suggest 6–10 finishes).
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M6.6, M8.1. Verification: the economy simulation reporting finishes-to-full-upgrade at the reference distribution.

### 12. Keyboard-only two-player is excluded without explanation and without a test either way

- **Severity:** minor (this is a documented design choice I disagree with, not a defect)
- **Location:** `PLAN.md` §Product commitments, line 13 ("four gamepads or keyboard plus three gamepads") and `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` §Split-screen and audio, line 108 ("Keyboard occupies at most one slot").
- **Problem.** Two players sharing one keyboard is therefore unsupported, even though the keyboard defaults in `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 9 already define both WASD and arrows. Nothing explains the omission, and no acceptance case covers or explicitly excludes it, so an implementer may "fix" it silently or omit it silently.
- **Proposed correction.** Either record the reason (input conflict and remapping complexity are plausible) or support a second keyboard binding set for two players. Either way, write the decision down. Implementing it would be a scope addition.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M3.1, M3 acceptance, T08.

### 13. No measurable acceptance criterion for visual or audio asset quality, and no fallback if no agent can hear the music

- **Severity:** minor
- **Location:** `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` §Geometry and graphics workflow, lines 63–67, and §Original music workflow, line 89.
- **Problem.** Asset review is defined as "review silhouette and readability, not only triangle count" using contact sheets and turntables — the right method, but with no rubric, no threshold, and no iteration budget. For music, the plan is admirably honest ("If tools cannot hear the result, report that limit and retain a playable preview for feedback; spectral statistics do not establish musical quality") but then defines no gate at all if that limit applies, so unreviewed music could pass as reviewed.
- **Failure example.** Twelve cars ship with distinct triangle counts and distinct palettes but three of them read as the same silhouette at split-screen distance, and nothing in the pipeline measured that. Six race themes ship that no agent or human ever listened to, because the only defined gates are objective meters.
- **Proposed correction.** Add a short, checkable rubric — road-edge contrast ratio against each palette, a missing-glyph and clipping check (already partly required), and a pairwise silhouette distinctness measure from turntable alpha renders with a stated ceiling. Cap review/fix cycles per asset family. For music, require at minimum one recorded human listen-through before release; if none is available, label the music explicitly as unreviewed in the release notes rather than passing the gate.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M5.4, M7.4, M8.6, M9.4.

### 16. The four-view draw-call budget is 2.6× the single-view budget, but nothing adapts LOD or resolution to player count

- **Severity:** minor
- **Location:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` §Performance budgets, line 133.
- **Problem.** The single-view budget is ≤250 draw calls and the four-view aggregate is ≤650, i.e. roughly 162 per view — about 35% tighter per view than single-player. Nothing ties LOD bias or 3D resolution scale to the number of active viewports, so an agent who meets the single-player gate can fail the four-player gate on identical content. (The triangle budgets are similarly asymmetric: 4 × 350k = 1.4M available versus a 900k cap.)
- **Proposed correction.** State that per-view LOD bias and/or 3D resolution scale are functions of active player count, and that the four-player gate is measured at default 1080p settings with the adaptation documented.
- **Affected milestones and verification.** M3.5, M8.3.

### 18. `CharacterBody3D` + a custom motor leaves the movement API unpinned

- **Severity:** minor
- **Location:** `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` §Movement and contacts, lines 62–70.
- **Problem.** "A custom arcade motor around `CharacterBody3D`, world sweeps, and ground probes" does not say whether the motor drives the body through `move_and_slide` or by writing velocity/transform directly. ### E1 — Split-screen listener and voice budget

- **Input.** An M1-era graybox: one `World3D`, four `SubViewport`s each with a `Camera3D`, eight cars with engine and tire sources, one full environment kit. Run on the recorded reference Mac.
- **Cost limit.** ≤2 agent-days. No new dependencies.
- **Pass/fail.** Exactly one current `AudioListener3D`, at the documented transform; measured simultaneous voices at peak within the allocation table; per-player gains match the manual relevance model within ±1 dB; frame time stays inside the p95 ≤16.7 ms gate.
- **Resolves.** Findings 2 and 3.

### E2 — Per-course lap-time feasibility

- **Input.** The six route recipes at their stated lengths, the slowest stock starter car, a scripted reference driver.
- **Cost limit.** ≤1 agent-day, after the M2 track compiler exists.
- **Pass/fail.** For every course, `length / measured lap time ≤ 0.80 ×` that car's measured top speed on that course. Any course failing blocks the track contract.
- **Resolves.** Finding 1.

### E3 — Loop-point accuracy and decoder cost for the chosen audio formats

- **Input.** One 90-second race theme and one 10-second engine loop, rendered at 48 kHz, exported as WAV and as the compressed candidate, played in a Godot loop scene for 20 minutes.
- **Cost limit.** ≤1 agent-day.
- **Pass/fail.** No audible or measurable click at the loop seam (compare the seam's sample discontinuity against an in-body discontinuity baseline); measured CPU cost per active voice within budget; the compressed format's loop offset lands within one sample of the intended point.
- **Resolves.** The compressed-loop question behind finding 3. This is a **hypothesis, not a confirmed problem**: the Godot class reference exposes `loop` and `loop_offset` on `AudioStreamOggVorbis` without stating seek precision, and I could not verify it from documentation alone.

### E4 — Roadstormer training throughput pilot

- **Input.** The headless Godot bridge, one trainable car on Riviera, frozen Classic opponents, PPO from random initialisation.
- **Cost limit.** Capped at 8 wall-clock hours **and** 100,000 decision steps, whichever comes first. No paid compute.
- **Pass/fail.** Measured decision steps per second per environment; the learning curve shows improvement over the behavioural-cloning initialisation within the cap; step count and wall time recorded.
- **Resolves.** Finding 6 — it supplies the number needed to decide whether M5 can require both styles, and whether the M4B budget is realistic at all.

### E5 — Generated-font scaling and coverage

- **Input.** The font generator's output, every HUD and menu string, at 100/125/150% scale, on all three OS targets.
- **Cost limit.** ≤1 agent-day.
- **Pass/fail.** No resampling artefacts at 150% (visual diff against a native 150% render), zero missing glyphs, no clipping, and a stated glyph coverage list.
- **Resolves.** Finding 7.

## Cross-document consistency

Substantive contradictions and missing shared contracts that could cause implementation agents to build incompatible systems. Ranked findings are referenced rather than repeated.

1. **Three undefined "reference" measurements.** The difficulty table (finding 5), Arcade Rush allowances (finding 9) and the upgrade-pacing criterion (finding 11) all require tuning "relative to" something that is never defined. This is the highest-leverage consistency gap in the set: all three should be defined the same way, as a recorded artefact with a content revision, so a single mechanism serves all of them.
2. **Reaction delay has no owner** (finding 4). `DriverCommand` is defined as sampled once per physics tick in `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 56, which is the right home for a delay line, but `OPPONENT_AI.md` line 25 places decisions at 5 Hz without saying the delay bypasses that layer.
3. **Movement API unpinned** (finding 18). `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 62 names `CharacterBody3D` without naming the driving API.
4. **Car contact proxy shape.** `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 80 lists "mass/contact proxy" as required Car information, and `ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` line 46 requires a "simple contact proxy", but neither states the shape, the origin convention, or whether it is a box, a hull, or a set of spheres. Combined with the custom resolver owning car-to-car response, two agents will produce different contact behaviour from identical meshes.
5. **Car coordinate conventions.** `ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` line 65 requires that "coordinate system, origin, forward direction, wheel pivot locations, and units" be specified "consistently" — but it is a requirement to specify, not a specification. Units are pinned (`GAME_DESIGN.md` line 35: metres, seconds, m/s) and forward follows Godot's −Z convention by implication, but the origin reference point and wheel pivots are not stated anywhere.
6. **Start-grid layout.** `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 81 requires the Track definition to contain the grid, and line 92 requires validating "eight-car grid space", but no document states the layout (columns, rows, spacing, stagger). An agent cannot generate it from the road definition alone.
7. **Behavioural cloning wording.** `OPPONENT_AI.md` line 35 says "Train small policies from random initialization on project-owned simulation data" while line 43 says behavioural cloning initialises the PPO actor. Read together they mean "no *external* pretrained weights", but the wording invites a reviewer to read a contradiction into the provenance claim. Worth a one-line clarification.
8. **"Rewards" is overloaded.** `GAME_DESIGN.md` line 47 says "Block rewards during immunity and until valid forward motion resumes", meaning drift style and surge credits; elsewhere "rewards" means credits and cup awards. A reader could implement immunity as blocking credit payouts.
9. **Time Attack gate validity.** `GAME_DESIGN.md` line 81 says "A lap is invalid if any required gate is missed" without defining which gates are required — all checkpoints, or only the start/finish pair.
10. **Four-view budget asymmetry** (finding 16) is also a cross-document gap: `GAME_DESIGN.md` line 192 sets HUD size targets per quadrant, while `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 133 sets a per-view geometry budget that no rule connects to player count.

No other shared counts disagreed. I verified the unlock graph (3 starters + 2 + 2 + 2 + 3 = 12 cars, matching `PLAN.md` line 15), the upgrade arithmetic (3 × 800 + 3 × 1600 = 7,200), the cup point table (10/8/6/5/4/3/2/1), the field composition for one and four humans (5+2 and 2+2 of 8), the five modes, the six courses used once each across three regional cups plus the six-race tour, and the three-lap race duration (60–95 s × 3 = 3.0–4.75 min, consistent with the "three to five minutes" target). Those all check out.

## Optional improvements

Kept separate from the necessary fixes above. Items marked **(changes scope)** alter the requested 1.0 feature set.

- **Use `Engine.get_license_info` / `Engine.get_copyright_info` at build time** to generate the in-game Licenses screen and the shipped notice files, rather than hand-maintaining a notice list. Verified from the Godot compliance documentation, which recommends exactly this so notices cannot go stale across engine versions. This makes M8.6 and T15 substantially cheaper and removes a whole class of attribution defect.

## Strong decisions to preserve

These are the choices I would not trade away, and the reasons.

1. **The runtime ownership table with its "Must not own" column** (`TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` lines 43–54). Naming what a component is forbidden to own is what prevents the classic arcade-racer failure where a camera, a particle effect or an audio cue changes a race result. Paired with `PLAN.md` line 40 ("An audiovisual improvement cannot change a race result") and the typed-event contract in line 58, this is the strongest single idea in the plan.
2. **The custom car-to-car contact resolver** (`TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 70): one coordinated system, relative swept hull motion evaluated from a shared tick-start snapshot, collision masks that exclude car-to-car so the resolver owns it, bounded mass-weighted response in stable participant order, capped positional correction. This is the correct design for eight cars and it rules out double engine response, vertical launching and order-dependent results in one stroke.
3. **Freezing the simulation during training bridge waits** (`OPPONENT_AI.md` line 39): one decision step advances exactly twelve 60 Hz ticks, then logical simulation and reward clocks freeze while the trainer works. This makes the environment step count exact and immune to IPC jitter — a detail most plans get wrong, and the reason the parity and reproducibility gates are achievable at all.
4. **Shipping weights as data with a GDScript inference implementation and a numeric parity test over ≥10,000 observations** (`OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 29 and 65). No runtime Python, PyTorch, ONNX or cloud service; playing never downloads a model. This is what makes the offline-play and small-runtime commitments real rather than aspirational.
5. **The provenance contract's refusal to invent metadata** (`ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` line 119: "Never set missing facts to an invented author or model. Mark unknown metadata explicitly") and line 125's "an agent cannot create legal authority through a generated sentence". These two sentences prevent the most likely way an LLM-produced public-domain project would make a false claim.
6. **"Do not replace a failed gate with a larger batch of generated content"** (`PLAN.md` line 214) and **"a successful bot race cannot establish that a drift is enjoyable"** (`PLAN.md` line 45). The plan repeatedly refuses to let automated verification stand in for experience review, and M8's acceptance explicitly says to report the absence of human playtest rather than call bot results proof of enjoyment.
7. **M0's "an untested platform does not become verified through a successful export"** (`PLAN.md` line 63) and M3's "If hardware is unavailable, keep this gate open and continue independent tooling rather than claiming completion" (line 101). Honest gate-keeping is rare in plans and expensive to retrofit.
8. **M2's requirement that a second agent generate an alternate test loop from documented inputs without editing generator code** (`PLAN.md` line 89). This is a real parameterisation test that most procedural-content plans skip, and it is the cheapest possible guard against the "procedural content feels repetitive" risk.
9. **Sampled-transform ghosts rather than input replay** (`TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` line 122, `GAME_DESIGN.md` line 81), with the explicit note not to assume input replay recreates physics exactly. Correct, and it avoids a whole category of ghost desync bug.
10. **Holding out a whole launch course plus unseen layouts from training** (`OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 51 and 81), with the rule that a simulator or handling revision invalidates affected results. This is what makes the "generalization" claim in the review prompt actually testable.
11. **Recording car ID, configuration hash, assists, course revision and handling revision with every record and ghost** (`GAME_DESIGN.md` line 81). Cheap now, impossible to add retroactively once records exist.
12. **The honest framing of AI authorship** (`PLAN.md` line 30): "Keep an authoring record instead of claiming that generated material has guaranteed originality or copyright status." This is the correct posture and it costs nothing.
