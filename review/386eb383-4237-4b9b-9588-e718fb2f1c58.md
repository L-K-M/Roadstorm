## Verdict

**Implementable after specific revisions.** The plan is unusually complete for a pre-implementation document: it fixes the engine, simulation ownership, content contracts, milestone order, licensing split, and verification matrix, and it correctly separates measurable gates from subjective experience review. The remaining gaps are concentrated in the learned-opponent contract, several acceptance metrics that are referenced but never defined, and a handful of career/race-rule thresholds. None of these appears to make the requested game impossible, but building without the revisions below would force implementation agents to invent shared contracts and would make key gates non-reproducible.

## Ranked findings

### F1 — Major: the learned-opponent observation/intent/action contract is undefined and is frozen after M4

- **Files/sections:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` §Architecture, lines 17–25; `PLAN.md` M4.2 line 108 and M4B.1 line 119.
- **Problem:** The architecture requires a Classic planner and a Storm policy to emit the same `TacticalIntent` (preferred lane/offset, target pace, follow/pass commitment, drift preference), then a common safety layer and shared path follower to consume it. The intent is named but not specified: no field types, units, ranges, coordinate frame, history length, action discretization, or normalization constants. `PLAN.md` defers freezing this contract to M4B.1, which runs after M4 has already implemented Classic planning, the safety layer, and the follower in M4.2.
- **Failure example:** The M4 agent implements `TacticalIntent.lane_offset` in metres and `target_pace` in m/s. The M4B agent assumes normalized `[-1,1]` offsets and a speed fraction. Integration then rewrites both sides and invalidates the M4 scenario evidence.
- **Proposed correction:** Define and version the contract before M4.2 — field list with units and ranges, local coordinate convention, history window, continuous versus discrete action head, and invalid-output behavior. Make M4.2 consume it. Reduce M4B.1 to validation and contract-version bumping rather than first definition.
- **Affected milestones:** M4, M4B, and downstream M7 retraining.
- **Verification needed:** Schema lint plus the already-planned parity test over at least 10,000 observations (`OPPONENT_AI.md` line 65).

### F2 — Major: learned style diversity is required but neither incentivized nor operationally defined

- **Files/sections:** `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 5, 27, 47–49, 71–72; `PLAN.md` M4B.5 line 123 and M4B.7 line 125.
- **Problem:** Behavioral cloning initializes from Classic demonstrations, and PPO rewards valid progress and clean passes. Nothing in the reward or architecture creates pressure for the two styles to differ from one another or from Classic behavior; two independent checkpoints can converge to near-identical policies. The evaluation gate then requires at least 25% “meaningful differences” per pair across 200 opportunity windows in each of six scenario families, but “meaningful” has no rubric, labeling procedure, or inter-rater check, and raw numeric differences are explicitly excluded.
- **Failure example:** Both checkpoints learn nearly the same safe following/passing policy. Reviewers disagree on whether small lane-change timing differences are “meaningful,” so the gate passes or fails by opinion rather than by a pre-agreed measure.
- **Proposed correction:** Add explicit style conditioning or a bounded diversity objective, or per-style risk/patience reward shaping with measurable targets. Pre-register the comparison rubric before training: which behaviors count, how windows are labeled, who labels them, and what agreement is required. Report per-style pass success, following duration, lane changes, contacts, and finish rates alongside the distinction score.
- **Affected milestones:** M4B, M7, M8.
- **Verification needed:** Pre-registered held-out evaluation, labeled window corpus, and independent clip review.

### F3 — Major: acceptance gates cite metrics and tolerances that are never defined

- **Files/sections:** `PLAN.md` M1 acceptance line 76; `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` T03 line 149 and T11 line 157; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 65; `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 97.
- **Problem:** M1 and T03 require 30/60/120 FPS benchmark results to stay within a “documented tolerance”; inference parity requires a “declared numeric tolerance”; difficulty is defined relative to a “validated safe reference pace” and a “documented reference driver”; deadline DNFs must be measured against “reference human pace ranges.” None of these quantities or their measurement methods appears anywhere in the plan.
- **Failure example:** One agent chooses a 0.5 m position tolerance and a 0.2% lap-time tolerance; another chooses 2 m and 1%. Both can truthfully claim M1/T03 compliance, and the difficulty percentages cannot be reproduced at all without the missing reference-driver definition.
- **Proposed correction:** Add a short metrics annex (or explicit fields in `TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md`) before M1 and M4B: exact tolerances, measurement procedure, how the reference driver/pace is constructed and recorded, and how human pace ranges are sampled. Version these values with handling and content revisions.
- **Affected milestones:** M1, M4, M4B, M8.
- **Verification needed:** A second agent re-runs the gates from the written values without asking the implementer.

### F4 — Major: career unlock thresholds and tie-break terms are ambiguous

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 161–172.
- **Problem:** Car awards depend on “completion,” “podium,” and “victory,” but only the points table (10/8/6/5/4/3/2/1) and a tie-break sequence are defined. “Podium” is not stated as top three or top four; “Thunderbrick on Storm Tour victory” can mean winning the cup standings or winning the final event; “wins” in the tie-break is undefined; and it is not stated whether tied places share awards.
- **Failure example:** One implementation awards Longshot for a top-three cup place and Thunderbrick for a first-place cup finish; another awards both at top four and for winning the final race. Players unlock different cars in the same number of events, and the T06 unlock-graph test can pass under either interpretation.
- **Proposed correction:** Define podium as shared place ≤3, victory as first in final cup standings after the stated tie-break, wins as race wins, and whether tied places share awards. Update the unlock table and tie-break text together.
- **Affected milestones:** M6 (T06), with M7 schedule data.
- **Verification needed:** Unlock-graph tests covering all twelve cars and constructed tie cases.

### F5 — Major: finish-deadline, host-wait, and DNF semantics contradict themselves

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 62–63; `PLAN.md` M4.1 line 107.
- **Problem:** The text simultaneously imposes a universal 45-second finish deadline, makes “wait for every human” the default for Rookie solo Career, retires AI after 45 seconds in that setting, adds a separate five-minute limit, and says unfinished cars receive DNF. It is unclear whether humans may continue past 45 seconds under host-wait, whether retiring AI are DNF with zero points, whether the five-minute cap ends the event, and what the default is outside Rookie solo Career.
- **Failure example:** In a Rookie solo career race, the human finishes at 50 seconds. One implementation records a valid finish because the host-wait setting applies; another records a DNF because the 45-second deadline is universal. Cup completion and points differ, and career unlocks stall.
- **Proposed correction:** Replace the prose with a small state table covering default versus wait-for-humans modes: expiry time per participant type, DNF and points treatment, ordering of unfinished cars, hard cap behavior, and the definition of a valid finish. State the default for each mode and difficulty.
- **Affected milestones:** M4, M6.
- **Verification needed:** Constructed T05 scenarios for deadline expiry, AI retirement, human overrun, and abandoned-event hang prevention.

### F6 — Major: learned-opponent feasibility is back-loaded onto the critical path

- **Files/sections:** `PLAN.md` lines 47–49, 115–127, 129–131; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` lines 45–47, 81.
- **Problem:** M4B depends on completed M4 and must finish before M5, yet the training-throughput pilot appears only inside M4B.2. If behavioral cloning plus PPO cannot produce safe, distinct styles on the available hardware within the capped budget, M5 is blocked or the launch scope must change late, after M6/M7 content decisions have begun.
- **Failure example:** M4 completes, M4B starts, and the measured bridge throughput supports only a fraction of the planned curriculum. The project must either extend the schedule, shrink the model/scenarios, or ship without a required feature — all decided after bulk-content planning is underway.
- **Proposed correction:** Run a thin end-to-end learned-AI spike in parallel with M4: frozen minimal contract, Riviera segment, 10,000–100,000 decision steps, capped wall time, three seeds. Gate the long M4B run and M5 entry on that measured result. Keep M4B formally dependent on M4, but start the experiment earlier.
- **Affected milestones:** M4, M4B, M5, M7.
- **Verification needed:** Throughput curve, completion/contact/safety statistics, and a style proxy against matched Classic baselines.

### F7 — Minor: difficulty reaction delays conflict with the 5 Hz tactical cadence

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 91–97; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 25.
- **Problem:** Tactical decisions run at 5 Hz (one decision per 200 ms), but the difficulty table specifies approximately 300/200/120 ms reaction delays. A 120 ms Expert reaction cannot be a decision-cadence effect at 5 Hz; if it is instead actuation smoothing or observation latency, the document does not say so.
- **Failure example:** An implementer applies a 120 ms delay to a 200 ms intent pipeline and measures no measurable difference from Racer, so Expert feels identical despite the intended later braking.
- **Proposed correction:** Define reaction delay precisely (observation delay, decision cadence, or control smoothing), state how it is measured, and reconcile it with the 5 Hz tactic rate. Either allow the low-level follower to react faster than the tactical layer or set Expert no lower than the tactical interval.
- **Affected milestones:** M4, M4B.
- **Verification needed:** Timestamped AI telemetry showing the chosen interpretation.

### F8 — Minor: the drift anti-farming rule is qualitative and untestable as written

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 29–31; `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` T02 line 148.
- **Problem:** Qualifying drift requires a “real signed course turn,” forward progress, speed, and maintained slip angle, while straight-road weaving, donuts, reversing, wall contact, and repeated resets are excluded. No numeric thresholds or fixtures are given. T02 requires boundary tests with no weave/donut/wall farming, but the scenarios needed to test that are undefined.
- **Failure example:** An agent sets a minimum curvature of 0.001 rad/m and accepts gentle S-weaves on a straight; another requires a minimum slip-angle integral and rejects them. The surge exploit surface differs, and T02 passes in both cases.
- **Proposed correction:** Specify the qualifying rule numerically (minimum signed curvature integral or heading change, minimum speed, slip-angle band, minimum duration) and the disqualifying events. Add named fixtures for weave, donut, wall ride, reset spam, and backwards driving. Similar qualitative thresholds exist for the drafting cone, wrong-way detection, major-impact classification, and the “up to 4%” surge; put these constants in versioned tuning resources with documented ranges.
- **Affected milestones:** M1, M4.
- **Verification needed:** Boundary tests in T02 and the M1 drift/grip comparison.

### F9 — Minor: checkpoint spacing and the handling benchmark fixture are undefined

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` lines 116–124; `PLAN.md` lines 70–76; `docs/TECHNICAL_DESIGN.md` lines 88–96, 145–151.
- **Problem:** The plan requires ordered checkpoint gates, sector timing, and safe anchors, but gives no gate count, spacing rule, or maximum interval. M1’s headline 5% drift-versus-grip claim refers to “the benchmark sweeper,” which is never specified as a named, reproducible fixture.
- **Failure example:** Two agents build different benchmark corners and report different parity results; neither is wrong, but the M1 gate cannot be compared across milestones or reviewers. Gate-spacing choices also affect lap-validation tests and progress exploits.
- **Proposed correction:** Specify M1/M2 benchmark fixtures (straight length, sweeper radius, hairpin radius, grade, surface, elevation) and add a gate-spacing/progress rule to the track contract. Reference the fixture by stable ID in acceptance criteria.
- **Affected milestones:** M1, M2.
- **Verification needed:** Fixture-based T03 and T05 runs.

### F10 — Minor: humans-only exhibition field composition is unspecified

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 57; `docs/OPPONENT_AI.md` line 9; `PLAN.md` line 12.
- **Problem:** Quick Race may offer an optional humans-only local field of two to four cars, but neither document states how many Roadstormers (if any) appear in such a field. `PLAN.md`’s product-commitment summary describes eight-car fields only and omits this mode variant entirely.
- **Failure example:** One implementation fills a two-car humans-only race with two Roadstormers; another treats it as Classic-only. The mixed-field identity and badge behavior differ, and the roadmap and specification disagree about the mode.
- **Proposed correction:** State the family rule for small humans-only fields (for example, up to two Roadstormers scaled by field size, or Classic-only by default) and add the variant to the `PLAN.md` racing commitment.
- **Affected milestones:** M4, M6.
- **Verification needed:** Roster composition tests for two-, three-, and four-human fields.

### F11 — Minor: Time Attack’s medal reference car is unnamed

- **Files/sections:** `docs/GAME_DESIGN.md` line 81; `docs/ASSET_PRODUCTION.md` line 55.
- **Problem:** Medals are authored per course for “a stock balanced starter,” but no car is identified and no configuration hash is required in the event data. Garage records occupy separate lists, yet the medal baseline remains ambiguous.

## Feasibility experiments

These are proposals for the implementation phase, not work performed during this review.

1. **Driving benchmark and drift anti-farming fixture.**
   - **Inputs:** graybox straight, broad sweeper, hairpin, crest, jump; stock Sparrow/Comet/Slidewinder; scripted steering profiles; candidate surge thresholds.
   - **Cost limit:** bounding-box labor only; a few hours of headless simulation on the reference machine.
   - **Pass/fail:** clean drift and grip times through the named sweeper within 5%; no weave, donut, wall ride, reverse, or reset-spam sequence earns surge or style credits; 30/60/120 FPS fixed-tick results within the newly declared tolerance.
   - **Decision resolved:** whether M1 can close, and the exact tolerance/anti-farm constants needed by F3 and F8.
2. **Four-camera renderer and simulation-cost pilot.**
   - **Inputs:** representative Riviera section, eight cars, scenery stress scene, one to four cameras, Compatibility versus Mobile renderer.
   - **Cost limit:** one bounded session on the recorded Apple M1 and GTX-1650-class references.
   - **Pass/fail:** p95 ≤16.7 ms and p99 ≤25 ms at 1920×1080, simulation within the provisional 4 ms budget, triangles/draw calls within the stated budgets.
   - **Decision resolved:** renderer choice and whether the art/geometry plan can proceed before bulk content (F6-adjacent schedule risk).
3. **Learned-opponent thin-slice training spike.**
   - **Inputs:** frozen minimal observation/intent contract, Riviera segment, Classic demonstrations, behavioral cloning plus PPO, 10,000–100,000 decision steps, three seeds.
   - **Cost limit:** capped wall-clock budget and step count; local hardware only; no paid service.
   - **Pass/fail:** measured throughput supports the curriculum; policy completes the segment safely; safety interventions stay below the stated thresholds; a proxy metric shows Surge and Switch differing from Classic and from each other.
   - **Decision resolved:** whether the model scale, reward design, or curriculum must change before the full M4B run (F1, F2, F6).
4. **Economy and unlock-graph simulation.**
   - **Inputs:** reward table, cup schedule, upgrade prices, synthesized finish distribution from eighth to first across laps and difficulties.
   - **Cost limit:** pure calculation; hours, not compute.
   - **Pass/fail:** exactly twelve cars reachable; no car requires upgrade spending; one three-lap finish buys the first upgrade; roughly eight ordinary finishes fully upgrade one car; shortest legal event meets the credits-per-minute check.
   - **Decision resolved:** whether the credit constants and unlock thresholds in M6 need revision before implementation (F4).
5. **Release-rights and provenance dry run.**
   - **Inputs:** two or three generated assets, one toy trained checkpoint, one smoke export package, manifest and license validator.
   - **Cost limit:** one bounded packaging session.
   - **Pass/fail:** validator blocks unknown origin, missing hashes, unregistered fonts/models, and absent notices; offline Licenses screen is complete; package description distinguishes original material from engine/dependency terms.
   - **Decision resolved:** whether the release gate mechanics work before M9, and whether the provenance fields are sufficient (licensing strength preserved).

## Optional improvements

These are not required for a coherent implementation:

- Add a tiny authored set of “hero corner” references (for example, one sweepers, one hairpin, one climb) that persist across courses and make handling and course rhythm reviewable by name. This does not change scope.
- Extend the asset manifest to include a stable benchmark-fixture ID for handling and course contracts, so regressions can be attributed to a fixture rather than a description. This does not change scope.
- Automate loudness, peak, loop-continuity, and missing-glyph checks in CI so M5/M7 audio and typography reviews start from measured facts. The plan already requires these checks; this only moves them earlier.
- Publish anonymized evaluation clips and model cards with the release, which the plan already plans; consider also publishing the pre-registered evaluation rubric so players can audit the style-difference claim.

Recommendations that **change requested scope**, listed only for explicit owner decisions:

- Reducing the launch roster from two learned styles to one, or making Classic Only the default field, would reduce M4B cost and risk but removes a requested 1.0 feature. The current plan explicitly forbids relabeling a scripted personality as learned AI; any such reduction should be an owner-approved scope change, not an implementation shortcut.
- Deferring Roadstormers until after 1.0 would unblock M5 if the F6 spike fails, but it contradicts the stated launch scope and the default two-Roadstormer field.
- Adding online multiplayer, dynamic weather physics, or a public mod editor would expand scope and is correctly deferred by the plan.

## Strong decisions to preserve

- **One shared fixed-tick simulation, independent of cameras, audio, and UI.** This is the right architecture for four-player split-screen and for honest replay/ghost/telemetry work.
- **A custom arcade motor instead of Godot’s `VehicleBody3D`.** The plan documents why, and it is necessary for the requested drift feel and bounded contacts.
- **Drift-versus-grip parity with no boost button.** The 5% target and capped exit surge preserve accessible drifting without turning the game into boost management.
- **Offline, small, safety-constrained learned rivals with no runtime cloud dependency.** This keeps latency, reproducibility, licensing, and offline play tractable, and the safety-layer design is the correct separation of learning from physical limits.
- **The Unlicense/CC0 split with honest provenance.** The plan does not overclaim copyright or trademark clearance, records contributor and generator metadata, and requires exact third-party notices. This is a licensing strength.
- **Milestone gates before bulk content.** Requiring handling, course tools, four-camera cost, and learned-AI evidence before M7 prevents generated volume from concealing weak foundations.
- **A complete asset manifest and model-card process.** Stable IDs, hashes, recipes, evaluation reports, and release rights gates are unusually concrete for a pre-production plan.
- **The Classic Only comparison setting.** It supports player preference and gives the learned-AI evaluation a controlled baseline.
- **Explicit separation of automated verification from experience review.** Repeatedly stating that bots and tests cannot prove enjoyment is the correct epistemics for an LLM-produced game.
